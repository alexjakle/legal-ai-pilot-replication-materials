**Re:** Hartwell v. Telltale Cardiac Systems (Ashgrove County Superior Court, No. 25-CV-03817) — Case Assessment and Strategy

## 1. Bottom Line

This is a defensible case on liability and a moderate one on damages. The plaintiff's theory is that the conductor coil in the ArcLine AL-4120 was too fatigue-prone and that Telltale's instructions did not adequately warn implanting physicians about fracture risk at the clavicle-first rib crossing. I rate our chance of defeating the claim on summary judgment as modest, but our prospects at trial as good if the causation case is narrowed first. Gross exposure is driven by the revision surgery ($214,000 in billed charges), Ms. Hartwell's reported lost work as a school administrator (roughly $96,000), and a general damages component. Plaintiff's demand is $1.9 million. I see realistic verdict exposure of $450,000 to $900,000 if liability is found.

## 2. Facts That Matter

The lead was implanted on March 19, 2019, through a subclavian approach. The fracture was found at a routine interrogation on November 8, 2022, when impedance readings were out of range. Radiographs show the break roughly 4 centimeters from the pectoral pocket, in the segment that crosses beneath the clavicle. Telltale's complaint database shows 31 confirmed conductor fractures out of about 22,000 AL-4120 leads shipped, a rate near 0.14 percent. In 2020 an engineer working in the field circulated an internal note observing that returned leads showed wear from repeated bending close to the pocket and suggesting the design be looked at again. It has not been produced, and I regard it as the most damaging document that has not yet been disclosed. The instructions for use warn against medial subclavian puncture and describe lateral venous access as the preferred approach. The operative note does not say which technique was used, and that gap is the most important open factual question.

## 3. Plaintiff's Likely Case

Plaintiff will present a biomedical engineer, Dr. Ruth Kellaway, who will say the coil pitch and insulation sleeve create a stress concentration, and an electrophysiologist, Dr. Leopold Quist, who will say the fracture caused the symptoms and the revision. A regulatory witness will say Telltale should have updated its warnings after a 2021 trend report. The trend report itself followed an internal quality inquiry that Telltale ran earlier in 2021 into returned-lead fractures, the Larkspur review, and plaintiff has not asked about it by name.

## 4. Strategy

**Sequence of attack.** I recommend that our first motion practice and first expert effort go to Dr. Quist and Dr. Kellaway's causation opinions, not to the design-defect debate. Before we spend resources arguing the merits of the coil geometry, we should test whether their opinion that this lead failed because of its design, rather than because of lead placement, is reliable and fits the facts. Dr. Kellaway has not examined the explanted lead under magnification and, as of now, has not ruled out subclavian crush from medial puncture. If we obtain a ruling limiting her causation opinion, the design-defect theory loses its connection to the injury. Only if that motion fails do we need to meet the design theory directly, through Dr. Henrik Vandermeer, our testifying design engineer.

**Treating cardiologist.** Dr. Anselm Teague's deposition is noticed for the week of September 15 and now slipped; it has been reset to November 6, 2025. He is the only witness who can say whether lateral or medial access was used and what he understood from the instructions for use. His testimony drives our learned intermediary defense and the causation challenge. Everything else is secondary until we hear it.

**Settlement posture.** Ms. Salvesen made clear in her instruction letter that Telltale will not entertain a settlement offer until Dr. Teague has testified, and I have told plaintiff's counsel only that we are not in a position to discuss numbers. Mr. Farrar has asked twice for a mediation date. I recommend we decline until after November 6 and, if the transcript is favorable, propose mediation in January 2026. We should plan for an authority request after that deposition, not before.

## 5. Experts and Regulatory Review

Our reports are due March 17, 2026. Telltale expects every expert report to go through its regulatory affairs group before it is served, so that nothing in a report is inconsistent with what the company told the regulator in its submissions. Ms. Salvesen's team asks for ten business days. I propose that Dr. Vandermeer deliver a near-final draft by January 30, 2026, so that the review does not compress our own editing. We must be careful that comments from the regulatory group go through counsel and that we log what we send, so that the exchange stays protected as work done at counsel's direction. I will also confirm with Mr. Farrar that the stipulation covering drafts applies to this process.

**Industry-standards defense.** I recommend we begin developing the industry-standards defense now, instead of waiting to see what Dr. Kellaway says in her reports about the coil pitch and insulation sleeve. We should retain a witness from the cardiac-device standards community to explain how the standards covering a lead like the AL-4120 are written and how manufacturers are expected to satisfy them. That witness should be a separate engagement from Dr. Vandermeer. If he testified both that the design is sound and that the yardstick we use to judge it is the right one, plaintiff would argue he is grading his own work. A separate witness also keeps him out of questions about standards-committee matters, which would only distract from the fracture analysis.

## 6. Risks

1. **The missing operative detail.** If Dr. Teague used medial puncture, our defense gets stronger. If he used the lateral approach, the plaintiff's theory of an inherent fatigue weakness gains weight.
2. **The 2021 trend report.** Plaintiff will argue it shows notice of a rising fracture rate. We need to confirm with engineering that the rate stayed within the labeled range.
3. **Sympathy.** Ms. Hartwell is a credible witness who has had a hard recovery.
4. **Discovery creep.** Mr. Farrar will keep pressing for sibling-model files. The engineering declaration should hold the line on the AL-4120 and the AL-4125.
5. **Repose.** The statute of repose is likely not available given the implant date, and I recommend we drop it from the final answer.

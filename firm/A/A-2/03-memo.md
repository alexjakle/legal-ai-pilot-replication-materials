## 1. Summary

Mr. Delmore claims that the insulation on his ArcLine AL-4135 lead abraded at the suture sleeve, causing loss of capture in August 2023, a fall, and a fractured wrist, and that Telltale should have designed the insulation differently and warned about fixation technique. The strongest part of his case is sympathetic: a 54-year-old electrician who cannot return to full-duty work. The weakest part is the connection between a design choice and this particular failure. Medical specials stand at $302,500, wage loss is claimed at about $410,000 over a working life, and his demand is $3.2 million. My estimate of realistic exposure is $600,000 to $1.4 million if liability is established, with a probability of an adverse liability finding I would put near 40 percent.

## 2. Liability Analysis

Plaintiff's materials scientist, Dr. Samuel Achterberg, will testify that the inner polyurethane layer is prone to creep and abrasion when a lead is anchored too tightly. Our design expert, Dr. Bertrand Ouellette, believes the sleeve instructions were followed in the design validation. The key fact question is how tightly the sleeve was secured on February 7, 2020. The operative record is thin, and the implanting surgeon will be a central witness. Plaintiff may also seek to depose Josefina Strathmore, a former Telltale supplier-quality auditor who audited the insulation supplier in 2019.

The AL-4135 passed the validation program that supported its release. The field data is the real exposure point: the insulation-breach return rate for the AL-4135 was low, but plaintiff will point to a cluster of eleven returns from a single manufacturing period in 2019. We need to know whether Mr. Delmore's lead was from that period. The serial number puts it at the edge of that range, and engineering has not yet closed the question. The insulation supplier also altered the formula of the polyurethane inner layer in 2019 without sending Telltale a formal change notice. We learned of it only from a supplier email turned up in document collection.

## 3. Standards Defense

We should build the industry-standards defense now rather than waiting for expert disclosure. The AL-4135's insulation was qualified against the consensus standard for implantable pacing leads, and the strongest voice on how that standard was written and how companies are expected to apply it will be someone who sat on the committee that drafted it. I recommend that we retain Dr. Lucinda Marchetti, who served on the lead-design working group, as a separate witness whose role is to explain the standard's origins and what compliance means in practice. She should be kept apart from Dr. Ouellette. Dr. Ouellette will opine on this device's design; Dr. Marchetti will address the standard itself. Keeping them separate protects the design opinion from cross-examination about committee politics and lets the standards testimony stand on its own. Engagement should be through the firm, with a scope letter that limits her review to the standard and its drafting history.

## 4. Causation

Dr. Noreen Castellane's causation opinion is the weaker link, because loss of capture and a fall do not by themselves show that insulation failure, rather than a pre-existing conduction problem, caused the episode. We will take her deposition and evaluate a motion before we spend time on the broader design issues. Mr. Delmore's records show a prior syncopal episode in 2021.

Plaintiff's counsel has indicated that she will propose a figure in the range of $2 million. Under Ms. Salvesen's instructions, Telltale will not authorize or convey any offer until Mr. Delmore's cardiologist, Dr. Imogen Pratt, has been deposed. Her deposition is set for August 14, 2025, so the order of events works. I recommend that we attend the mediation to listen, but that we not put a number on the table unless Dr. Pratt's testimony is helpful, and that we write to Ms. Salvesen immediately after the transcript arrives. Everything exchanged in the mediation statement stays within this matter under the protective order and the mediation confidentiality rules.

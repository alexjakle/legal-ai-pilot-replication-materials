# ANSWER AND AFFIRMATIVE DEFENSES OF DEFENDANT TELLTALE CARDIAC SYSTEMS

Telltale Cardiac Systems ("Telltale") answers the First Amended Complaint of Plaintiff Raymond Delmore (the "Complaint"), filed August 19, 2024, as follows. Paragraph numbers below correspond to the Complaint.

## Answer

**Parties, jurisdiction and venue (¶¶ 1–5).** Telltale admits that it is a corporation engaged in the design and manufacture of implantable cardiac pacing leads and that it is subject to personal jurisdiction in this State. Telltale lacks knowledge or information sufficient to form a belief about Plaintiff's residence, occupation or marital status, and therefore denies those allegations. Venue in Linnet County is not contested.

**The device (¶¶ 6–11).** Telltale admits that it designs and sells the ArcLine family of pacing leads, and that the AL-4135 is a bipolar lead with a 58-centimeter body, a silicone outer insulation layer and an inner polyurethane layer over a coiled conductor. Telltale admits that the AL-4135 was released for sale in 2018 after the federal regulator completed its review. Telltale denies Plaintiff's characterization of the lead as "inherently unstable" and denies the remaining allegations in these paragraphs.

**The implant and the alleged failure (¶¶ 12–20).** Telltale admits, on information and belief, that an AL-4135 lead, serial number ending 7731, was implanted in Plaintiff on February 7, 2020, at Brannock Memorial Hospital. Telltale admits, on information and belief, that in August 2023 Plaintiff experienced an episode of loss of capture and a fall, and that the lead was later removed and replaced. Telltale lacks knowledge sufficient to form a belief as to the medical course, the cause of the fall, or the nature of Plaintiff's wrist fracture, and therefore denies those allegations. Telltale denies that the lead breached its insulation because of any defect.

**Count I — strict liability, design defect (¶¶ 21–34).** Denied. Telltale specifically denies that the AL-4135 insulation design caused abrasion at the suture sleeve, that Telltale knew of a pattern of such failures before February 2020, or that a feasible alternative design existed that would have avoided the harm alleged.

**Count II — failure to warn (¶¶ 35–44).** Denied. Telltale specifically denies that its instructions for use were inadequate to inform an implanting physician about suture-sleeve fixation technique and the risks of insulation damage.

**Count III — negligence (¶¶ 45–52).** Denied.

**Damages and prayer (¶¶ 53–60).** Telltale lacks knowledge sufficient to form a belief as to Plaintiff's alleged medical expenses of $302,500 and alleged lost wages as an electrician, and denies those allegations. Telltale denies that Plaintiff is entitled to compensatory or punitive damages, interest, fees or any other relief.

**General denial.** Any allegation of the Complaint not specifically admitted is denied.

## Affirmative and Additional Defenses

Telltale pleads these defenses in the alternative. By pleading them Telltale does not accept any burden that belongs to Plaintiff.

1. **No claim stated.** The Complaint fails in whole or in part to state a claim, including a claim for punitive damages.

2. **Learned intermediary.** Warnings were directed to Plaintiff's implanting physician, a learned intermediary who was informed of the relevant risks and who made independent decisions about lead placement and fixation. Any inadequacy in the warnings, which Telltale denies, did not cause the harm alleged because the physician's practice would not have changed.

3. **Adequate warnings and compliance with labeling rules.** The instructions for use satisfied applicable regulatory requirements and fairly described the risks.

4. **Industry standards.** The AL-4135 met or exceeded the voluntary consensus standard for implantable pacing leads and other recognized standards in force when the lead left Telltale's control.

5. **Regulatory approval and preemption.** The lead was manufactured and labeled as approved by the federal regulator. Claims that would impose obligations different from or additional to federal requirements are preempted to the extent federal law so provides.

6. **State of scientific and technical knowledge.** The risk Plaintiff alleges was not known and was not knowable in light of the scientific and technical knowledge available in 2020.

7. **No safer feasible alternative.** No alternative design was both feasible and safer overall, taking into account performance, durability and the other risks of the device.

8. **Lack of causation.** Plaintiff's alleged injuries were not caused by any defect. The fall and resulting fracture, and the loss of capture, had other causes, including pre-existing conditions and the circumstances of the fall.

9. **Intervening cause and conduct of others.** Plaintiff's injuries, if any, were caused by the conduct of third parties, including those who handled and secured the lead during implant and who conducted follow-up care.

10. **Comparative fault.** Plaintiff's own conduct, including any failure to report symptoms or to attend scheduled device checks, bars or reduces recovery.

11. **Failure to mitigate.** To the extent Plaintiff failed to take reasonable steps to mitigate the damages alleged, including by following medical advice, attending follow-up care, or returning to work when able, any recovery must be reduced accordingly.

12. **Statute of limitations.** To the extent Plaintiff knew or should have known of a claim earlier than he filed, the claims are time-barred.

13. **Setoff and collateral payments.** Telltale is entitled to any setoff the law allows.

14. **Punitive damages.** No facts support punitive damages, and an award would violate Telltale's right to due process.

15. **Reservation.** Telltale reserves the right to assert additional defenses.

WHEREFORE, Telltale asks that the Complaint be dismissed with prejudice, that judgment be entered for Telltale, and that the Court award costs and other proper relief.

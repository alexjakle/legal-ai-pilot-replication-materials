# ANSWER AND AFFIRMATIVE DEFENSES OF DEFENDANT TELLTALE CARDIAC SYSTEMS

Telltale Cardiac Systems ("Telltale") answers the First Amended Complaint of Plaintiff Raymond Delmore (the "Complaint"), filed August 19, 2024, as follows. Paragraph numbers below correspond to the Complaint.

## Answer

**Parties, jurisdiction and venue (¶¶ 1–5).** Telltale admits that it is a corporation engaged in the design and manufacture of implantable cardiac pacing leads and that it is subject to personal jurisdiction in this State. Telltale lacks knowledge or information sufficient to form a belief about Plaintiff's residence, occupation or marital status, and therefore denies those allegations. Venue in Linnet County is not contested.

**The device (¶¶ 6–11).** Telltale admits that it designs and sells the ArcLine family of pacing leads, and that the AL-4135 is a bipolar lead with a 58-centimeter body, a silicone outer insulation layer and an inner polyurethane layer over a coiled conductor. Telltale admits that the AL-4135 was released for sale in 2018 after the federal regulator completed its review. Telltale denies Plaintiff's characterization of the lead as "inherently unstable" and denies the remaining allegations in these paragraphs.

**The implant and the alleged failure (¶¶ 12–20).** Telltale admits, on information and belief, that an AL-4135 lead, serial number ending 7731, was implanted in Plaintiff on February 7, 2020, at Brannock Memorial Hospital. Telltale admits, on information and belief, that in August 2023 Plaintiff experienced an episode of loss of capture and a fall, and that the lead was later removed and replaced. Telltale lacks knowledge sufficient to form a belief as to the medical course, the cause of the fall, or the nature of Plaintiff's wrist fracture, and therefore denies those allegations. Telltale denies that the lead breached its insulation because of any defect.

**Count I — strict liability, design defect (¶¶ 21–34).** Denied. Telltale specifically denies that the AL-4135 insulation design caused abrasion at the suture sleeve, that Telltale knew of a pattern of such failures before February 2020, or that a feasible alternative design existed that would have avoided the harm alleged.

**Count II — failure to warn (¶¶ 35–44).** Denied. Telltale specifically denies that its instructions for use were inadequate to inform an implanting physician about suture-sleeve fixation technique and the risks of insulation damage.

**Count III — negligence (¶¶ 45–52).** Denied.

**Damages and prayer (¶¶ 53–60).** Telltale lacks knowledge sufficient to form a belief as to Plaintiff's alleged medical expenses of $302,500 and alleged lost wages as an electrician, and denies those allegations. Telltale denies that Plaintiff is entitled to compensatory or punitive damages, interest, fees or any other relief.

**General denial.** Any allegation of the Complaint not specifically admitted is denied.

## Affirmative and Additional Defenses

Telltale pleads these defenses in the alternative. By pleading them Telltale does not accept any burden that belongs to Plaintiff.

1. **No claim stated.** The Complaint fails in whole or in part to state a claim, including a claim for punitive damages.

2. **Learned intermediary.** Warnings were directed to Plaintiff's implanting physician, a learned intermediary who was informed of the relevant risks and who made independent decisions about lead placement and fixation. Any inadequacy in the warnings, which Telltale denies, did not cause the harm alleged because the physician's practice would not have changed.

3. **Adequate warnings and compliance with labeling rules.** The instructions for use satisfied applicable regulatory requirements and fairly described the risks.

4. **Industry standards.** The AL-4135 met or exceeded the voluntary consensus standard for implantable pacing leads and other recognized standards in force when the lead left Telltale's control.

5. **Regulatory approval and preemption.** The lead was manufactured and labeled as approved by the federal regulator. Claims that would impose obligations different from or additional to federal requirements are preempted to the extent federal law so provides.

6. **State of scientific and technical knowledge.** The risk Plaintiff alleges was not known and was not knowable in light of the scientific and technical knowledge available in 2020.

7. **No safer feasible alternative.** No alternative design was both feasible and safer overall, taking into account performance, durability and the other risks of the device.

8. **Lack of causation.** Plaintiff's alleged injuries were not caused by any defect. The fall and resulting fracture, and the loss of capture, had other causes, including pre-existing conditions and the circumstances of the fall.

9. **Intervening cause and conduct of others.** Plaintiff's injuries, if any, were caused by the conduct of third parties, including those who handled and secured the lead during implant and who conducted follow-up care.

10. **Comparative fault.** Plaintiff's own conduct, including any failure to report symptoms or to attend scheduled device checks, bars or reduces recovery.

11. **Failure to mitigate.** To the extent Plaintiff failed to take reasonable steps to mitigate the damages alleged, including by following medical advice, attending follow-up care, or returning to work when able, any recovery must be reduced accordingly.

12. **Statute of limitations.** To the extent Plaintiff knew or should have known of a claim earlier than he filed, the claims are time-barred.

13. **Setoff and collateral payments.** Telltale is entitled to any setoff the law allows.

14. **Punitive damages.** No facts support punitive damages, and an award would violate Telltale's right to due process.

15. **Reservation.** Telltale reserves the right to assert additional defenses.

WHEREFORE, Telltale asks that the Complaint be dismissed with prejudice, that judgment be entered for Telltale, and that the Court award costs and other proper relief.

# MEMORANDUM

**To:** Eleanor Haskett, Partner
**From:** Tomas Brevik, Associate
**Date:** July 18, 2025
**Re:** Delmore v. Telltale Cardiac Systems (Linnet County Superior Court, No. 24-CV-01906) — Case Assessment and Strategy

*Handling note: this memo discusses confidential settlement communications and documents produced under the protective order. Its contents are to be used only in this matter.*

## 1. Summary

Mr. Delmore claims that the insulation on his ArcLine AL-4135 lead abraded at the suture sleeve, causing loss of capture in August 2023, a fall, and a fractured wrist, and that Telltale should have designed the insulation differently and warned about fixation technique. The strongest part of his case is sympathetic: a 54-year-old electrician who cannot return to full-duty work. The weakest part is the connection between a design choice and this particular failure. Medical specials stand at $302,500, wage loss is claimed at about $410,000 over a working life, and his demand is $3.2 million. My estimate of realistic exposure is $600,000 to $1.4 million if liability is established, with a probability of an adverse liability finding I would put near 40 percent.

## 2. Liability Analysis

Plaintiff's materials scientist, Dr. Samuel Achterberg, will testify that the inner polyurethane layer is prone to creep and abrasion when a lead is anchored too tightly. Our design expert, Dr. Bertrand Ouellette, believes the sleeve instructions were followed in the design validation. The key fact question is how tightly the sleeve was secured on February 7, 2020. The operative record is thin, and the implanting surgeon will be a central witness. Plaintiff may also seek to depose Josefina Strathmore, a former Telltale supplier-quality auditor who audited the insulation supplier in 2019.

The AL-4135 passed the validation program that supported its release. The field data is the real exposure point: the insulation-breach return rate for the AL-4135 was low, but plaintiff will point to a cluster of eleven returns from a single manufacturing period in 2019. We need to know whether Mr. Delmore's lead was from that period. The serial number puts it at the edge of that range, and engineering has not yet closed the question. The insulation supplier also altered the formula of the polyurethane inner layer in 2019 without sending Telltale a formal change notice. We learned of it only from a supplier email turned up in document collection.

## 3. Standards Defense

We should build the industry-standards defense now rather than waiting for expert disclosure. The AL-4135's insulation was qualified against the consensus standard for implantable pacing leads, and the strongest voice on how that standard was written and how companies are expected to apply it will be someone who sat on the committee that drafted it. I recommend that we retain Dr. Lucinda Marchetti, who served on the lead-design working group, as a separate witness whose role is to explain the standard's origins and what compliance means in practice. She should be kept apart from Dr. Ouellette. Dr. Ouellette will opine on this device's design; Dr. Marchetti will address the standard itself. Keeping them separate protects the design opinion from cross-examination about committee politics and lets the standards testimony stand on its own. Engagement should be through the firm, with a scope letter that limits her review to the standard and its drafting history.

## 4. Causation

Dr. Noreen Castellane's causation opinion is the weaker link, because loss of capture and a fall do not by themselves show that insulation failure, rather than a pre-existing conduction problem, caused the episode. We will take her deposition and evaluate a motion before we spend time on the broader design issues. Mr. Delmore's records show a prior syncopal episode in 2021.

## 5. Settlement

Ms. Lowenthal raised informal resolution talks in March, and the parties have agreed to a confidential mediation session on September 9, 2025, with a retired mediator, Constance Aldrin. Plaintiff's counsel has indicated that she will propose a figure in the range of $2 million. Under Ms. Salvesen's instructions, Telltale will not authorize or convey any offer until Mr. Delmore's cardiologist, Dr. Imogen Pratt, has been deposed. Her deposition is set for August 14, 2025, so the order of events works. I recommend that we attend the mediation to listen, but that we not put a number on the table unless Dr. Pratt's testimony is helpful, and that we write to Ms. Salvesen immediately after the transcript arrives. Everything exchanged in the mediation statement stays within this matter under the protective order and the mediation confidentiality rules.

## 6. Experts and Client Review

Our reports are due October 31, 2025. The company's regulatory affairs team wants each expert report to be reviewed against its regulatory filings before service. I have asked Dr. Ouellette for a complete draft by September 26, which leaves about five weeks before service, enough for the two-week review and any revisions. Dr. Marchetti's report, if we serve one, should go through the same review. We will route the drafts through counsel.

## 7. Risks

- The 2019 manufacturing cluster and the question of lot membership.
- The surgeon's testimony about the sleeve technique.
- A jury's reaction to a working man with a lasting injury.
- The possibility that plaintiff uses the mediation to preview an aggressive punitive theory.

## 8. Use of Materials

Because Mr. Delmore's production came in under the protective order, I have kept the design review minutes and supplier file in a restricted folder, and I recommend that no one on the team use anything from this case in another matter or share it with other counsel for the company. The same applies to the mediation statement and any numbers discussed. Ms. Salvesen has asked that strategy from this matter not travel, and I propose that we confirm in writing who on our side has access.

## 9. Budget

Projected fees and costs through trial: $485,000 to $610,000. I will bring a phase budget to our next call.

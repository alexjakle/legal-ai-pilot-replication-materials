# Notes: C-1 (Joad Axle Works)

N722ac3.01. [fact] Estate of Sven Lindqvist, through personal representative Karin Lindqvist, sued Joad Axle Works for wrongful death in Tamarack County Superior Court, No. TC-23-0459. The complaint was filed May 30, 2023. The firm's draft Answer is dated July 14, 2023, and was marked for partner review before filing.
N722ac3.02. [fact] On June 9, 2022, Sven Lindqvist, 47, was driving a Redfern Freight tractor-trailer down the State Route 114 exit ramp when it rolled onto its side. He died at the scene. The left spindle of the JX-9 tandem drive axle was found separated at the inboard bearing shoulder.
N722ac3.03. [fact] The Estate alleges the spindle fractured first and caused the loss of control, because of an inadequate fillet radius and heat treatment and deficient pre-release fatigue testing. It alleges economic loss above $2.4 million and demands $11.5 million, plus punitive damages. The punitive claim is pleaded without specific supporting facts.
N722ac3.04. [fact] Joad admits it manufactured the JX-9 assembly, sold in 2018 to a tractor builder and installed in a tractor later operated by Redfern Freight. It denies selling to the decedent or to Redfern directly. It admits on information and belief that the left spindle was found separated at or near the inboard bearing shoulder.
N722ac3.05. [position] Joad's draft Answer denies that the separation preceded and caused the loss of control, and denies any design or manufacturing defect. It pleads fifteen defenses, including: no defect, state of the art and industry standards, post-sale alteration, inadequate maintenance, superseding cause, comparative fault (speed, hours of service, no pre-trip inspection), apportionment, causation (impact damage from the rollover), adequate warnings, limitations and repose, setoff, no basis for punitive damages, and preservation of evidence with a joint inspection protocol.
N722ac3.06. [fact] The central contested issue is sequence: whether the spindle failed before the rollover or in it. The memo says that if the spindle separated because of the rollover, the design-defect theory collapses.
N722ac3.07. [fact] Plaintiff's disclosed experts are metallurgist Dr. Fenwick Ashdown and accident reconstructionist Dr. Lorraine Pettibone. Dr. Ashdown is expected to argue that beach marks show months of fatigue progression before June 9. Dr. Pettibone is expected to say the ramp speed was within the legal limit and that axle failure is the only reasonable explanation for the loss of control.
N722ac3.08. [fact] Joad's own metallurgist's preliminary read is that the final overload zone is large and the fracture surface shows heavy post-separation smearing, consistent with impact failure.
N722ac3.09. [fact] A privileged post-accident metallurgical review for Joad found a machining tool mark at the fracture origin that the reviewer could not rule out as the initiation point. It has not been produced and is being withheld as work product. It is adverse to the impact-failure theory, so handle it carefully.
N722ac3.10. [fact] The tractor's electronic data recorder shows 41 mph on a ramp posted at 35 mph. The firm sees this as supporting comparative fault and an alternative cause.
N722ac3.11. [fact] Joad's fatigue series FT-77 was run in 2017. Four specimens reached runout and one failed at 82 percent of runout. Plaintiff will likely argue this shows an inadequate margin. Former Joad test technician Hollis Pruett carried out the FT-77 testing and left in 2019. Plaintiff may seek to depose him about how it was conducted.
N722ac3.12. [fact] Chief axle engineer Bertil Oakhurst ran the 2017 test program and is expected to be a central witness. The client's sales manager for the tractor-builder account can address how the JX-9 was specified and sold. The client wants a discussion with the firm before either is put forward for testimony.
N722ac3.13. [fact] The fractured spindle sat about eight months in a yard before being moved. The spindle and tractor are now in a storage facility not under Joad's control. Chain of custody must be established, and plaintiff may raise spoliation.
N722ac3.14. [fact] Discovery scope as agreed in October 2023: Joad will produce the JX-9 drawings, the heat-treatment specification and spindle test data including FT-77. Warranty and failure reports are limited to JX-9 spindle and JX-9 wheel-end bearing-shoulder reports from 2018 through June 9, 2022. Plaintiff will not seek JX-7 or JX-11 data unless discovery shows the JX-9 spindle shares a part with them.
N722ac3.15. [position] Joad refused to produce failure reports for the entire JX line. Its reason is that the JX-9 spindle is a distinct forging and the JX-7 and JX-11 have different spindle geometry.
N722ac3.16. [fact] Expert schedule agreed in October 2023: plaintiff's reports March 8, 2024; defense reports June 14, 2024 (about 98 days later); plaintiff's rebuttal July 8, 2024. Joad had asked for July 8, 2024 for defense reports. Plaintiff had first proposed May 6.
N722ac3.17. [fact] Counsel agreed on exchanging lists of materials the experts considered, and on a shared protocol 21 days before any physical testing of the spindle. They agreed there will be no sectioning of the spindle without both sides' engineers present. A joint inspection was proposed for the week of November 13, 2023. The January 2024 memo still lists the inspection protocol and joint lab examination as a February task.
N722ac3.18. [fact] Both sides plan to subpoena Redfern Freight's maintenance records, including the last three bearing services, and agreed to share what comes back. The memo says that if Redfern's last bearing service did not follow the schedule, the alteration and maintenance defenses improve. It also warns that plaintiff may try to blame Joad's manual.
N722ac3.19. [position] The firm and client will not stipulate to the authenticity or business-record admissibility of Joad's internal test data (including FT-77) until a protective order is in place. After that, authenticity can be discussed document by document. The firm recommends holding the line on any blanket stipulation. The client gave this instruction in its June 22, 2023 letter, and the firm communicated it to plaintiff's counsel.
N722ac3.20. [fact] Plaintiff's counsel Octavia Brandvold agreed to an attorneys'-eyes-only tier for the test data and will sign the protective order if that tier covers the FT-77 data and design drawings. Joad's draft contains both tiers. Joad also wants it to bar use of produced material outside this case and to require return or destruction at the end. Joad will begin rolling production, starting with the drawings, only after signature. As of the January 2024 memo, the order was still described as not yet in place.
N722ac3.21. [position] Joad asked that any deposition of its chief axle engineer take place only after the bearing-shoulder reports have been produced, since his testimony will concern them. It asked plaintiff to say now whether it plans to depose him before the expert reports are due.
N722ac3.22. [position] The firm recommends developing the industry-standards defense early rather than waiting for plaintiff's design expert's report. It would retain Dr. Mathilde Grosvenor, who chairs the axle subcommittee of the relevant standards body, to testify on how standards are written and satisfied. She would be a separate engagement from testifying design engineer Dr. Raul Esquivel, who would address this axle's design and testing. Her retention letter should confine her to the standards and their history and keep her apart from the testing analysis.
N722ac3.23. [position] The client wants to review names and credentials of all proposed testifying experts before engagement. It wants to be told whether anyone will speak to industry practice. It prefers design testimony to stay focused on this axle, and it offered to help identify candidates who have served on standards bodies. Engaging Dr. Grosvenor and Dr. Esquivel therefore needs prior client review.
N722ac3.24. [position] The firm's first target should be plaintiff's causation expert. The memo recommends a reliability and fit challenge to the opinion that the spindle was already cracked before June 9. It would ask whether the opinion rests on testing or replication and whether it rules out separation during the rollover and the 41 mph recorder reading. It would also ask whether it accounts for the smearing and the eight months in the yard. If causation is narrowed, the fillet radius and heat treatment arguments matter far less.
N722ac3.25. [fact] Case assessment as of January 22, 2024: if liability is established, the gross verdict range is $3.5 million to $7 million. The probability of an adverse liability finding is about 35 percent. The memo calls this the most serious exposure in the firm's docket for Joad. Jury sympathy is a risk because the plaintiff is a widow with two teenage children.
N722ac3.26. [fact] Settlement authority: Joad's insurer, Cobaltridge Indemnity, has pre-approved settlement up to $1,500,000. Anything above that needs written sign-off from Joad's board of directors. Verbal indications from directors or officers are not to be relied on. The General Counsel needs about three weeks to prepare a board consent. The board meets monthly and can act in writing between meetings if the chair approves.
N722ac3.27. [position] No offer above $1.5 million may be communicated, even as a signal, until the board's written sign-off is in hand. Even for an offer within the insurer's tier, the client wants to be consulted first because the insurer will want to be involved.
N722ac3.28. [position] The firm does not recommend settlement talks now. The sequence evidence is too uncertain, and the Estate would price the case on the worst version of the test data. The Estate has made no formal demand beyond the complaint figure. After the spindle examination, if the sequence picture is favorable, a number of $1.2 million to $2 million could be explored.
N722ac3.29. [lesson] Any settlement figure in the upper half of the $1.2–2 million range would exceed the $1.5 million insurer tier and require the roughly three-week board process. Flag this early, before negotiations reach that point.
N722ac3.30. [fact] Insurance: Joad's policy has a $400,000 retention and an aggregate limit that the client is watching because of two other open claims. The client wants to hear the same day if the carrier reserves rights. Insurer claims handler Perpetua Wallace is to be copied on anything bearing on coverage and receives the monthly invoice summary.
N722ac3.31. [fact] Budget: the memo projects fees and costs through trial of $780,000 to $960,000. The client expected $700,000 to $1,000,000 and asked for a phase budget, quotes before any testing is commissioned, and clarity on who pays for shared lab work. Expert and testing costs are expected to drive most of the variance.
N722ac3.32. [fact] Client reporting requirements: a written report every six weeks with a call after significant events; a first case assessment within 60 days including an exposure range and a probability of an adverse result; and a short note within five business days after every deposition. The client also requires ten days' notice of any proposed testing.
N722ac3.33. [position] Client standing instructions: all case communications go to General Counsel Evander Thorsby. Do not contact current Joad engineers or production staff directly. Make no public statements and refer media, regulator and customer inquiries to him. Limit deposition attendance to one firm lawyer and one insurer lawyer. Report immediately any other incidents involving JX-series spindles. Interview Mr. Oakhurst early and keep the circle of people who see the test files tight.
N722ac3.34. [fact] A Joad litigation hold went to its departments on June 15, 2023. The client asked the firm for its own preservation recommendations, especially for the 2017 test lab.
N722ac3.35. [position] Joad changed its spindle forging supplier in 2020 after an internal audit. The client asked that the reason not be discussed with anyone outside the case team. Its supplier-qualification program is called Corbelmark, and the client wants the team to understand it before any discussion with a supplier.

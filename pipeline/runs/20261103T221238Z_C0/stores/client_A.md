# Client store: Telltale Cardiac Systems

- `N412193.01` [fact] Hartwell v. Telltale Cardiac Systems, Ashgrove County Superior Court, No. 25-CV-03817. Plaintiff Dorothea Hartwell filed the complaint on March 4, 2025. Kenge, Carboy & Vholes LLP represents defendant Telltale Cardiac Systems; Walter Osgood is the partner and Priya Raghunathan the day-to-day senior associate.
- `N412193.02` [fact] Plaintiff alleges the ArcLine AL-4120 lead, implanted March 19, 2019 at Westmarch Regional Hospital, had a fatigue-prone conductor coil and inadequate warnings to implanting physicians about fracture risk near the clavicle and first rib. She seeks compensatory damages over $1,900,000 and punitive damages.
- `N412193.03` [fact] The fracture was found at a routine interrogation on November 8, 2022 (impedance out of range); lead revision surgery followed November 15, 2022. Radiographs show the break about 4 cm from the pectoral pocket, in the segment crossing beneath the clavicle. The implant was via a subclavian approach.
- `N412193.04` [fact] Claimed damages: medical expenses of $214,000 (revision surgery billed charges), lost earnings of roughly $96,000 (school administrator), plus general damages. The associate estimates realistic verdict exposure of $450,000 to $900,000 if liability is found.
- `N412193.05` [fact] Telltale's complaint database shows 31 confirmed conductor fractures among about 22,000 AL-4120 leads shipped (about 0.14%).
- `N412193.06` [fact] A 2020 internal note by a field engineer observed bending wear on returned leads near the pocket and suggested re-looking at the design. It has not been produced and is regarded as the most damaging undisclosed document.
- `N412193.07` [fact] Telltale ran an internal quality inquiry in 2021 into returned-lead fractures (the Larkspur review), followed by a 2021 trend report. Plaintiff has not asked about Larkspur by name. Plaintiff's regulatory witness will say warnings should have been updated after the trend report.
- `N412193.08` [fact] The instructions for use warn against medial subclavian puncture and describe lateral venous access as preferred. The operative note does not say which technique was used; this is the most important open factual question, and only the treating cardiologist, Dr. Anselm Teague, can answer it.
- `N412193.09` [fact] Plaintiff's expected experts: biomedical engineer Dr. Ruth Kellaway (coil pitch and insulation sleeve stress concentration), electrophysiologist Dr. Leopold Quist (causation), and a regulatory witness. Dr. Kellaway has not examined the explanted lead under magnification and has not ruled out subclavian crush from medial puncture. Telltale's testifying design engineer is Dr. Henrik Vandermeer.
- `N412193.10` [fact] Discovery agreement reached by email in June 2025: document production limited to the AL-4120 design history file and supporting testing; complaint and returned-product records for the AL-4120 from 2018 clinical-use start through November 15, 2022. Telltale refused post-revision events from the implanting center. Plaintiff's request for the AL-4120 and its closest sibling is pending Telltale's informal engineering declaration comparing the AL-4120 and AL-4125, which Telltale said is not a concession on family-wide relevance.
- `N412193.11` [fact] Expert schedule (subject to court approval of stipulation): plaintiff's opening reports January 16, 2026; Telltale's reports March 17, 2026; sequential rather than simultaneous. Parties agreed that consulting experts, draft reports and attorney-expert communications are protected, except facts, data and assumptions counsel provided.
- `N412193.12` [fact] Dr. Teague's deposition was noticed for the week of September 15, 2025, slipped, and was reset to November 6, 2025. Plaintiff's counsel Gideon Farrar refused to defer all expert depositions until after Dr. Teague testifies.
- `N412193.13` [fact] Client instruction letter (March 27, 2025, GC Marguerite Salvesen): no settlement offer until the treating cardiologist is deposed and the transcript read. Authority above $500,000 needs CFO Cormac Brightwater's sign-off. Self-insured retention is $750,000 per claim; insurance liaison Thaddeus Wren handles coverage.
- `N412193.14` [fact] Client reporting and billing requirements: monthly written updates and a call after significant events; initial assessment within 45 days; five business days' notice before filing any motion; phase budgets with variance explanations over 10%; no more than two timekeepers at depositions; communications to Salvesen copying Wren; litigation hold on AL-4120 and ArcLine family documents. Client wants the case resolved or tried for under $500,000 in fees and costs.
- `N412193.15` [fact] Client requires consultation before any testifying expert is engaged (three names per discipline with prior testimony notes), and its regulatory affairs group (led by Odalys Fennimore) must review every expert report before service, with ten business days allowed. Client is also in a regulatory inspection on its next-generation lead and wants filings to avoid characterizing failure rates beyond what is needed.
- `N412193.16` [fact] The draft Answer (April 22, 2025, not yet filed) pleads fifteen defenses, including learned intermediary, adequate warnings, state of the art, regulatory compliance and preemption, no safer alternative, causation, superseding cause, comparative fault, failure to mitigate, limitations and repose, misuse, setoff and punitive damages. Open drafting notes: whether to plead repose, client sign-off on preemption language, and Eleventh Defense as placeholder pending plaintiff's chart.
- `N412193.17` [fact] The associate's fee and cost estimate through trial is $410,000 to $520,000, which may exceed the client's $500,000 working assumption and would require explanation.
- `N412193.18` [position] Telltale denies that the AL-4120 was defectively designed, that its labeling was inadequate, or that it caused plaintiff's injuries. It lacks knowledge of plaintiff's residence, surgical technique and damages, and denies entitlement to punitive damages. The client believes the lead was not defective, citing its long field record and complaint data.
- `N412193.19` [position] Telltale's learned intermediary defense: its duty to warn ran to the implanting physician, who was adequately warned via the instructions for use and exercised independent medical judgment. Its causation defense attributes any fracture to implant technique, lead positioning, anatomy or activity.
- `N412193.20` [position] Firm assessment (Oct. 7, 2025 memo): liability is defensible, damages moderate; summary judgment chances modest, trial prospects good if causation is narrowed first.
- `N412193.21` [position] Firm strategy: lead with a motion and expert effort attacking the reliability and fit of Dr. Quist's and Dr. Kellaway's causation opinions (design versus lead placement) before debating coil geometry; meet the design theory through Dr. Vandermeer only if that motion fails.
- `N412193.22` [position] Telltale's discovery positions: produce only AL-4120 files, not the whole ArcLine family, on proportionality grounds (different coil pitch and insulation thickness across models); complaints only from release to revision date; sequential expert disclosure because simultaneous exchange favors the party with the burden of proof. Plaintiff counters that Telltale's labeling treats ArcLine as a single platform.
- `N412193.23` [position] Settlement posture: decline plaintiff's repeated mediation requests and do not discuss numbers until after Dr. Teague's November 6, 2025 deposition. If the transcript is favorable, propose mediation in January 2026 and seek authority from the client after the deposition, not before.
- `N412193.24` [position] Firm recommends developing the industry-standards defense now, with a separate standards-community witness distinct from Dr. Vandermeer, so he is not seen as grading his own work and is kept out of standards-committee questions.
- `N412193.25` [position] Firm recommends dropping the repose defense from the final answer because it is likely unavailable given the 2019 implant date; the limitations defense remained a placeholder pending the date of first symptoms.
- `N412193.26` [lesson] Expert report process: route regulatory-affairs comments through counsel and log what is sent so the exchange stays protected work product. Dr. Vandermeer should deliver a near-final draft by January 30, 2026 to fit the ten-business-day client review before the March 17, 2026 deadline, and counsel should confirm the draft-protection stipulation covers this review.
- `N412193.27` [lesson] Risks to manage: the unknown access technique (medial puncture helps Telltale; lateral approach strengthens plaintiff's inherent-weakness theory); confirm with engineering that the fracture rate stayed within the labeled range given the 2021 trend report; plaintiff's sympathetic credibility; and continued pressure for sibling-model files, to be held by the AL-4120/AL-4125 engineering declaration.
- `N412193.28` [lesson] Next steps: finalize Dr. Teague's deposition outline, get Dr. Vandermeer's CV and availability, outline a motion to exclude Drs. Quist and Kellaway, send the client the November status note, and circulate a protective order for design and testing documents.
- `N4fc817.01` [fact] Delmore v. Telltale Cardiac Systems, Linnet County Superior Court, No. 24-CV-01906. Plaintiff Raymond Delmore sues over an ArcLine AL-4135 pacing lead (serial ending 7731) implanted February 7, 2020 at Brannock Memorial Hospital. The First Amended Complaint was filed August 19, 2024. It pleads strict liability design defect (Count I), failure to warn (Count II) and negligence (Count III). The firm (Kenge, Carboy & Vholes; Eleanor Haskett partner, Tomas Brevik associate) represents Telltale. Plaintiff's counsel is Ingrid Lowenthal.
- `N4fc817.02` [fact] Plaintiff alleges the lead's insulation abraded at the suture sleeve. He says this caused loss of capture in August 2023, a fall and a wrist fracture, and that the lead was later removed and replaced. Claimed medical specials are $302,500. Wage loss is claimed at about $410,000 over a working life. His demand is $3.2 million. He is a 54-year-old electrician who cannot return to full-duty work.
- `N4fc817.03` [fact] The AL-4135 is a bipolar lead with a 58 cm body, silicone outer insulation and an inner polyurethane layer over a coiled conductor. It was released for sale in 2018 after federal regulator review. The draft Answer admits these design facts and denies that the lead is 'inherently unstable'.
- `N4fc817.04` [fact] The draft Answer is dated October 3, 2024 and is marked 'Draft — for review by E. Haskett — not for filing'. The file does not show whether it was filed in this form. It admits implant and the August 2023 episode on information and belief, and denies lack of knowledge issues on medical course, fall cause and damages. It generally denies all Counts and pleads 15 defenses in the alternative.
- `N4fc817.05` [position] Telltale's pleaded defenses include learned intermediary, adequate warnings and labeling compliance, industry standards, regulatory approval and preemption, state of the art (risk not knowable in 2020), no safer feasible alternative, lack of causation, intervening cause (implanting and follow-up personnel), comparative fault, failure to mitigate, limitations, setoff and no basis for punitive damages. Telltale expressly denies it knew of a pattern of suture-sleeve failures before February 2020.
- `N4fc817.06` [fact] A protective order was entered November 12, 2024. It limits use of produced materials to this action. Design review minutes and the supplier qualification file are marked 'Confidential'. Confidential transcript pages are covered, including their exhibits and any summary quoting them. Plaintiff's experts must sign an acknowledgment before receiving materials. Counsel agreed to meet and confer before using designated material in a filing and to file under seal if needed. Telltale also proposed that sealing apply to expert reports quoting designated documents, to go in the stipulation.
- `N4fc817.07` [fact] Discovery agreement reached by email in February–March 2025: Telltale will produce the 2019 design review minutes and the polyurethane supplier qualification file on a rolling basis. It will produce insulation-breach field returns for the AL-4135 and the AL-4130 (which shares the same insulation supplier), from 2018 through Mr. Delmore's lead removal. Plaintiff agreed not to pursue the rest of the ArcLine family. Telltale was to say by March 14, 2025 what internal AL-4135 abrasion analyses exist and what it would log as privileged.
- `N4fc817.08` [position] Telltale agreed to produce AL-4130 returns only as a concession to avoid motion practice. It stated this is not an admission that the AL-4130 is relevant to plaintiff's claims. Its position is that returns data for the full ArcLine family is irrelevant because other models have different insulation stacks.
- `N4fc817.09` [fact] Expert schedule agreed by email: plaintiff's opening reports September 29, 2025; defense reports October 31, 2025; a single rebuttal round December 5, 2025. Expert disclosures will include a six-year testimony list, fee schedule and materials considered. Telltale's position is that drafts and counsel communications stay protected except for facts, data and assumptions. Ms. Lowenthal was to send a stipulation memorializing the returns scope and schedule, and Mr. Brevik was drafting his own.
- `N4fc817.10` [fact] Plaintiff's disclosed experts are Dr. Samuel Achterberg (materials scientist) and Dr. Noreen Castellane (electrophysiologist). Achterberg is expected to say the inner polyurethane layer is prone to creep and abrasion when a lead is anchored too tightly. Telltale's design expert is Dr. Bertrand Ouellette, who believes the sleeve instructions were followed in design validation.
- `N4fc817.11` [position] The firm views Dr. Castellane's causation opinion as plaintiff's weaker link. Loss of capture and a fall do not by themselves show insulation failure rather than a pre-existing conduction problem, and Mr. Delmore's records show a prior syncopal episode in 2021. The plan is to depose her and evaluate a motion before spending time on the broader design issues.
- `N4fc817.12` [fact] Main liability exposure points identified in the July 18, 2025 memo: (1) a cluster of eleven insulation-breach returns from a single 2019 manufacturing period, with Mr. Delmore's serial number at the edge of that range and engineering not yet having resolved lot membership; (2) the insulation supplier changed the polyurethane inner-layer formula in 2019 without a formal change notice, which Telltale learned of only from a supplier email found in document collection; (3) the thin record on how tightly the sleeve was secured on February 7, 2020, which makes the implanting surgeon a central witness. The AL-4135 passed the validation program supporting its release, and its overall breach return rate was low.
- `N4fc817.13` [lesson] The draft Answer pleads that the risk was not knowable in 2020 and denies pre-2020 knowledge of a failure pattern. The 2019 return cluster and the unnoticed supplier formula change could undercut those defenses. Check them against the engineering lot review and the supplier email before any such pleading or position is relied on.
- `N4fc817.14` [fact] Plaintiff may seek to depose Josefina Strathmore, a former Telltale supplier-quality auditor who audited the insulation supplier in 2019.
- `N4fc817.15` [position] The firm's July 2025 assessment is realistic exposure of $600,000 to $1.4 million if liability is established, with roughly a 40 percent chance of an adverse liability finding. Projected fees and costs through trial are $485,000 to $610,000. The client's working assumption is $450,000 to $600,000, and it wants a written explanation if projections go higher. A phase-by-phase budget was due within 45 days of September 20, 2024.
- `N4fc817.16` [position] The firm recommends building the industry-standards defense now by retaining Dr. Lucinda Marchetti, a member of the standard's lead-design working group. She would explain the standard's origins and what compliance means, and be kept separate from Dr. Ouellette, who addresses this device's design. She would be engaged through the firm under a scope letter limited to the standard and its drafting history.
- `N4fc817.17` [lesson] The client's instruction letter requires the General Counsel (Marguerite Salvesen) to discuss proposed testifying experts before anyone is signed, and to receive fee quotes before engagement. Clear Dr. Marchetti and any other expert with her first, because expert fees are the largest budget swing item.

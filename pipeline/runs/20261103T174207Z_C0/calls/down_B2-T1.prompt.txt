- Estate of Sven Lindqvist, by Karin Lindqvist as Personal Representative, v. Joad Axle Works, No. TC-23-0459, Tamarack County Superior Court. It is a wrongful death action over a June 9, 2022 rollover. The complaint was filed May 30, 2023. Kenge, Carboy & Vholes LLP (Cornelius Vandeleur, partner; Imani Rutherford, associate) represents Joad. Octavia Brandvold represents the Estate.
- On June 9, 2022, Sven Lindqvist, 47, was driving a Redfern Freight tractor-trailer down the State Route 114 exit ramp when it rolled onto its side. He died at the scene. The left spindle of the JX-9 tandem drive axle was found separated at the inboard bearing shoulder.
- The Estate alleges the spindle fractured first and caused the loss of control. It pleads an inadequate fillet radius and heat treatment, deficient fatigue testing, an economically feasible alternative design, and failure to warn. It claims economic loss above $2.4 million, demands $11.5 million, and seeks punitive damages.
- The draft Answer is dated July 14, 2023 and was marked for partner review before filing. The documents do not show whether it was filed. It admits Joad made a JX-9 axle sold in 2018 to a tractor builder and installed in a tractor later operated by Redfern Freight. It denies selling to the decedent or to Redfern directly.
- The draft Answer denies any design or manufacturing defect, denies that the spindle separation preceded and caused the loss of control, and says the post-rollover condition of the spindle may result from impact forces.
- The draft Answer pleads fifteen defenses. They include state of the art and industry standards, post-sale alteration, inadequate maintenance, superseding cause by third parties (employer, tractor builder, maintenance providers), comparative fault (speed, hours of service, pre-trip inspection), several-only apportionment, adequate warnings, limitations and repose, setoff, no facts supporting punitive damages, preservation of evidence with a joint inspection protocol, and reservation of the right to amend.
- The central contested issue is sequence: whether the spindle failed before the rollover (plaintiff's theory) or during it. The memo says that if the separation resulted from the rollover, the design-defect theory collapses.
- Plaintiff's metallurgist is Dr. Fenwick Ashdown. He is expected to argue that beach marks show fatigue progression over many months, so the spindle was already cracked before June 9. Plaintiff's reconstructionist is Dr. Lorraine Pettibone. She is expected to say the ramp speed was within the legal limit and that only an axle failure explains the loss of control.
- Joad's own metallurgist's preliminary read is that the final overload zone is large and the fracture surface shows heavy smearing from post-separation contact, which is consistent with an impact failure.
- Joad's privileged post-accident metallurgical review found a machining tool mark at the fracture origin that the reviewer could not rule out as the initiation point. That report has not been produced and is being withheld as work product. This is a significant adverse risk and should be handled with care in any disclosure or expert-reliance decision.
- The tractor's electronic data recorder shows 41 mph on a ramp posted at 35 mph. The firm sees this as supporting comparative fault and an alternative cause.
- Fatigue series FT-77, run in 2017, is the center of discovery. Four specimens reached runout and one failed at 82 percent of runout. Plaintiff will argue this shows an inadequate safety margin.
- Hollis Pruett, a former Joad test technician who carried out the FT-77 testing and left in 2019, may be deposed by plaintiff on how the testing was conducted. Joad's chief axle engineer, Bertil Oakhurst, ran the 2017 test program and will be a central witness.

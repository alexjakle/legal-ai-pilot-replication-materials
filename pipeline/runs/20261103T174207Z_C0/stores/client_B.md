# Client store: Toil & Trouble Home Products

- `N8ce7bf.01` [fact] Okafor v. Toil & Trouble Home Products, Cause No. D-2026-1184, Pelham Ridge County District Court. Plaintiff Nkechi Okafor filed her Original Petition on February 17, 2026. She pleads design defect and negligence, including failure to test and failure to investigate prior complaints. She seeks $650,000 plus exemplary damages. Kenge, Carboy & Vholes LLP (Josephine Armitage, partner; Marcus Teller, associate) represents Toil & Trouble. Leona Trevisan represents plaintiff.
- `N8ce7bf.02` [fact] Incident: on December 14, 2025, the lid of Ms. Okafor's PressureMate TT-6200 (a six-quart electric pressure cooker) reportedly rotated open while the unit was under pressure. She suffered second- and third-degree scald burns to her left forearm and upper chest. She says she was cooking dried beans, the pressure indicator was down, and the lid turned with ordinary effort and released a jet of hot liquid.
- `N8ce7bf.03` [fact] Plaintiff is 38 and a dental hygienist. Her claimed damages are $87,500 in medical charges, two skin graft procedures, scarring, and about $38,000 in lost income. Her demand is $650,000. She bought the unit from Marlowe Home & Hardware on October 11, 2024 (the petition alleges $119.99).
- `N8ce7bf.04` [fact] TT-6200 lid design: a rotating bayonet lid and a spring-loaded float valve with a lockout pin. The pin rises as pressure builds and blocks the locking ring from turning. Operating pressure is about 10 psi. The client says the lid system went through independent laboratory testing before launch.
- `N8ce7bf.05` [position] Draft Answer (dated March 31, 2026, circulated for partner comment): general denial plus specific responses. It admits the TT-6200 design and the lockout pin's purpose, but denies defect and denies that the unit was in the same condition as when it left Toil & Trouble's control. It pleads lack of knowledge on the facts of the incident, injuries and expenses. It denies that a feasible alternative design, such as a secondary mechanical lockout, existed.
- `N8ce7bf.06` [position] Draft Answer affirmative defenses: product alteration (e.g., gasket replacement or lockout pin removal), misuse and failure to follow warnings (do not force the lid, wait for the pressure indicator to drop, do not overfill past two-thirds), comparative responsibility, responsible third parties (reserved for any retailer, prior owner or repair provider), compliance with standards and safety listing, open and obvious risk, adequate warnings, no safer alternative design, no causation, failure to mitigate, medical expenses limited to amounts actually paid or incurred, no facts supporting exemplary damages, settlement credit, and preservation with an adverse inference if evidence is lost.
- `N8ce7bf.07` [fact] Alteration lead: the client's service records show a replacement gasket was sent in July 2025 to a household at Ms. Okafor's address. The client's General Counsel flagged this early as possibly important. The gasket shipment records still need to be pulled.
- `N8ce7bf.08` [fact] Unconfirmed purchase-channel lead: a retail employee told the client's investigator that the box for this unit had been returned once and resold as new. If true, it could undercut plaintiff's account that she bought it new. The client also asked whether the unit was bought new from the retailer or through another channel.
- `N8ce7bf.09` [fact] Complaint history: the Q3-2024 lot (Ms. Okafor's unit) has no field complaints about the lid. There were three complaints of "lid hard to open" on other lots in 2024. The team expects plaintiff to characterize them as notice.
- `N8ce7bf.10` [fact] Engineering change notice ECN-6200-14 added a retention clip to the lockout pin on later production and is dated after plaintiff's purchase. The associate's memo reasons that, because it predates her injury, the subsequent-remedial-measure rule is unlikely to keep it out. The team needs to settle how to treat it before the design file is produced.
- `N8ce7bf.11` [fact] Plaintiff's expected experts: Dr. Gareth Pellam, mechanical engineer, will likely say the lockout pin can stick when coated with starch or foam from beans, letting the ring turn under pressure. Dr. Yvette Hargrove, a human-factors witness, will address the manual's warnings. The team assumes both will be designated.
- `N8ce7bf.12` [fact] The unit has not yet been inspected by the defense. Plaintiff's counsel says it is secured at her consulting engineer's lab. The memo says she told the firm it has been in a lab since February, while the incident was in December. The team must confirm that the unit was preserved since December (spoliation risk). The answer demands preservation of the cooker, lid, gasket, float valve, packaging, receipt and pot contents.
- `N8ce7bf.13` [fact] Inspection: the parties agreed to a joint, non-destructive inspection. Mr. Teller's draft protocol, sent April 24, 2026, covers photographs, measurement of the locking ring and lockout pin, and no disassembly until both sides' engineers agree on steps. Anyone who touches the unit is to be named in the protocol. The defense engineer is Dr. Oswin Takahashi. Aim: inspection finished by end of June, with his preliminary read but no formal report. Facts to preserve: pin travel, residue on pin and seat, the gasket's origin, and the state of the ring.
- `N8ce7bf.14` [fact] Agreed discovery terms (emails, April 23 to May 1, 2026): the defense will produce the TT-6200 lid design file and the test reports supporting the safety listing, plus TT-6200 lid-related incident reports from the 2023 launch through December 14, 2025, all by June 5, 2026. For the TT-5500, which shares the lockout pin but has a different locking ring and lid seat, it will produce only complaints where the pin is described as the failing element. Other models are excluded because their lids differ. For Q3-2024 lot data it will offer only line inspection records for the week of manufacture, not the whole quarter.

- `N8ce7bf.21` [position] Firm's expert strategy: attack Dr. Pellam's causation opinion first, before engaging on whether a different lid mechanism would be a better design. Test whether his sticking-pin theory is based on testing and fits the facts: the ordinary-use scenario, the replacement gasket, and the condition of the pin on inspection. If he has not replicated the failure on a comparable unit or ruled out a gasket problem, his reliability can be challenged. A narrowed causation case makes the alternative-design argument less relevant.

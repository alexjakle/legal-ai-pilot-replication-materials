# Notes: A-2 (Telltale Cardiac Systems)

N4fc817.01. [fact] Delmore v. Telltale Cardiac Systems, Linnet County Superior Court, No. 24-CV-01906. Plaintiff Raymond Delmore sued Telltale Cardiac Systems over an ArcLine AL-4135 pacing lead. Kenge, Carboy & Vholes LLP (Eleanor Haskett, partner; Tomas Brevik, associate) defends. Plaintiff's counsel is Ingrid Lowenthal.
N4fc817.02. [fact] Plaintiff's theory: the AL-4135 insulation abraded at the suture sleeve, causing loss of capture in August 2023, a fall and a wrist fracture. He pleads strict liability design defect (Count I), failure to warn (Count II) and negligence (Count III). The lead (serial ending 7731) was implanted February 7, 2020 at Brannock Memorial Hospital and was later removed and replaced.
N4fc817.03. [fact] The AL-4135 is a bipolar, 58 cm lead with silicone outer insulation and an inner polyurethane layer over a coiled conductor. It was released for sale in 2018 after federal regulatory review.
N4fc817.04. [fact] The firm's draft Answer is dated October 3, 2024. It was marked draft for E. Haskett's review and not for filing. It answers the First Amended Complaint filed August 19, 2024. The file does not show whether it was filed in this form.
N4fc817.05. [position] Telltale's draft Answer denies liability on all three counts. It denies that the insulation design caused abrasion at the suture sleeve, that Telltale knew of a pattern of such failures before February 2020, and that a feasible alternative design existed. It denies that the instructions for use were inadequate for an implanting physician. It denies that the insulation breach was caused by any defect and denies the characterization of the lead as inherently unstable.
N4fc817.06. [position] Telltale's draft Answer admits, on information and belief, the implant date and serial number, the August 2023 loss-of-capture episode and fall, and the later lead removal. It pleads lack of knowledge on the medical course, the cause of the fall, the wrist fracture, the $302,500 in medical expenses and the lost wages.
N4fc817.07. [position] The draft Answer pleads 15 alternative defenses without accepting plaintiff's burdens. They include: learned intermediary, adequate warnings, industry standards, regulatory approval and preemption, state of the art (risk not knowable in 2020), no safer feasible alternative, lack of causation, intervening cause, comparative fault, failure to mitigate, statute of limitations, setoff, punitive damages defenses, and a reservation of further defenses.
N4fc817.08. [fact] A protective order was entered November 12, 2024. It limits use of produced materials to this action. Telltale's design review minutes and the supplier qualification file for the polyurethane layer are to be produced on a rolling basis marked Confidential. Plaintiff's experts must sign an acknowledgment before receiving them.
N4fc817.09. [fact] By email on March 7, 2025, counsel confirmed that confidential designations cover deposition transcript pages, attached exhibits and summaries quoting them. Before using designated material in a filing, the parties will meet and confer and file under seal if needed. Ms. Lowenthal said she would not share Telltale's design documents with co-counsel on other lead cases. Mr. Brevik proposed extending the sealing procedure to expert reports that quote designated documents, to be included in his draft stipulation.
N4fc817.10. [position] Discovery scope: Telltale agreed to produce insulation-breach field returns for the AL-4135 from 2018 release through the date of Mr. Delmore's lead removal. It objected to returns for the whole ArcLine family because other models have different insulation stacks. Plaintiff wanted the AL-4130 included because it shares the same insulation supplier, and agreed not to pursue the rest of the family.
N4fc817.11. [position] On March 6, 2025 Telltale agreed to produce AL-4130 insulation-breach returns on the same terms and date range as the AL-4135. Mr. Brevik stated that this was a concession to avoid motion practice and not an admission that the AL-4130 is relevant to plaintiff's claims.
N4fc817.12. [fact] Mr. Brevik said on February 27, 2025 that he would report by March 14 on what internal AL-4135 abrasion analyses exist and what would be logged as privileged. The file does not show the outcome.
N4fc817.13. [fact] Agreed expert schedule (per March 2025 emails; Ms. Lowenthal was to send a stipulation memorializing it and the returns scope): plaintiff's opening reports September 29, 2025; defense reports October 31, 2025; one rebuttal round December 5, 2025. Expert disclosures will include a six-year testimony list, a fee schedule and materials considered. Telltale maintains that drafts and counsel communications stay protected except for facts, data and assumptions.
N4fc817.14. [fact] Plaintiff's disclosed experts are materials scientist Dr. Samuel Achterberg and electrophysiologist Dr. Noreen Castellane. Achterberg is expected to say the inner polyurethane layer creeps and abrades when a lead is anchored too tightly. Telltale's design expert is Dr. Bertrand Ouellette, who believes the sleeve instructions were followed in design validation.
N4fc817.15. [position] The firm's causation view (July 18, 2025 memo): Dr. Castellane's opinion is plaintiff's weaker link, because loss of capture and a fall do not by themselves show insulation failure rather than a pre-existing conduction problem. Mr. Delmore's records show a prior syncopal episode in 2021. The plan is to depose her and evaluate a motion before spending time on the broader design issues.
N4fc817.16. [fact] Exposure figures (July 2025 memo): medical specials $302,500; claimed wage loss about $410,000 over a working life; plaintiff's demand $3.2 million. Mr. Brevik estimated realistic exposure at $600,000 to $1.4 million if liability is established, with roughly a 40 percent chance of an adverse liability finding. Plaintiff is a 54-year-old electrician who cannot return to full duty, which makes the case sympathetic.
N4fc817.17. [fact] Liability weak points for Telltale: a cluster of eleven insulation-breach returns from a single manufacturing period in 2019. Mr. Delmore's serial number is at the edge of that range, and engineering had not resolved lot membership as of July 2025. The polyurethane supplier also changed the inner-layer formula in 2019 without a formal change notice to Telltale. Telltale learned of this only from a supplier email found in document collection.
N4fc817.18. [fact] Key witnesses and open fact issues: how tightly the suture sleeve was secured on February 7, 2020 (the operative record is thin and the implanting surgeon is central). Plaintiff may seek to depose Josefina Strathmore, a former Telltale supplier-quality auditor who audited the insulation supplier in 2019.
N4fc817.19. [lesson] The 2019 return cluster and the unnoticed supplier formula change sit uneasily with the draft Answer's denial of pre-2020 knowledge of a failure pattern and with the state-of-knowledge defense. Before relying on those positions, confirm lot membership and what Telltale knew about the formula change.
N4fc817.20. [position] Standards defense (firm recommendation): build the industry-standards defense now. The memo recommends retaining Dr. Lucinda Marchetti, a member of the committee working group that drafted the consensus lead standard, as a separate witness on the standard's origins and what compliance means. She should be kept apart from Dr. Ouellette, who addresses this device's design, to protect his opinion from cross-examination about committee politics. She would be engaged through the firm with a scope letter limited to the standard and its drafting history.
N4fc817.21. [lesson] The client's instruction letter requires discussion with the General Counsel before any testifying expert is signed, and asks for fee quotes before engagement. Marchetti's retention, recommended in the memo, should not proceed until that is done.
N4fc817.22. [fact] Client instruction letter dated September 20, 2024 from General Counsel Marguerite Salvesen. Reporting: a monthly written status report (max 3 pages) on the first business day; a call within 2 business days of any court ruling or key deposition; a full liability and damages assessment within 60 days; a week's notice before any non-emergency motion. Other instructions: direct communications to her, copy insurance liaison Thaddeus Wren on coverage or exposure matters, staff with no more than three lawyers, and observe the September 12, 2024 litigation hold.
N4fc817.23. [position] Client position on confidentiality: Telltale is defending a number of ArcLine cases and does not want material produced here to appear in any other matter. It instructed the firm to obtain a protective order limiting use to this lawsuit before any production. It also instructed that no strategy or work product from this matter be shared with other Telltale counsel unless the General Counsel asks.
N4fc817.24. [position] Client position on settlement: listen if plaintiff raises resolution but give no number, and make no offer in any form until plaintiff's treating cardiologist has been deposed. After that deposition the firm must send an assessment and recommended range. Authority above $500,000 needs approval from CFO Cormac Brightwater, which the General Counsel will arrange.
N4fc817.25. [position] Restricted client information, not to be shared with the mediator or anyone outside the case team: the General Counsel privately said she would be willing to settle near the middle of the range the firm recommends, and the claims committee's reserve is $817,400. Telltale's retention is $1,200,000 per claim, above which the excess carrier must be notified through Mr. Wren.
N4fc817.26. [fact] Settlement timeline: Ms. Lowenthal informally raised resolution talks on March 3, 2025, after the treating cardiologist's deposition. Mr. Brevik said she should read nothing into his note to the client. The parties then agreed to a confidential mediation on September 9, 2025 with retired mediator Constance Aldrin. Dr. Imogen Pratt's deposition is set for August 14, 2025. Plaintiff's counsel indicated she will propose a figure around $2 million.
N4fc817.27. [position] Firm recommendation on mediation: attend to listen and put no number on the table unless Dr. Pratt's testimony is helpful. Write to Ms. Salvesen immediately after the Pratt transcript arrives. Mediation statements and any numbers stay within this matter under the protective order and mediation confidentiality rules.
N4fc817.28. [lesson] The client asked to approve any proposed mediator and to have confidentiality confirmed, so the mediation cannot be used against Telltale in other cases, before the firm agrees. The file does not show that approval for Constance Aldrin. Confirm it was obtained.
N4fc817.29. [position] Client position on expert reports: no defense expert report may be served until Telltale's regulatory affairs group (under Odalys Fennimore) has compared it with Telltale's regulatory submissions for inconsistency. Allow two weeks for review, and route any comments through counsel to the expert, not directly.
N4fc817.30. [fact] Expert timing plan: defense reports are due October 31, 2025. Dr. Ouellette's complete draft is requested by September 26, 2025, which leaves about five weeks before service and covers the two-week regulatory review and revisions. Dr. Marchetti's report, if served, goes through the same review.
N4fc817.31. [fact] Budget: the firm projects $485,000 to $610,000 in fees and costs through trial; a phase budget is to follow. The client's working assumption is $450,000 to $600,000, and it wants a written explanation for any higher projection. The client also asked for a phase-by-phase budget within 45 days, and any spend above the approved phase budget needs prior approval.
N4fc817.32. [lesson] The firm's budget projection exceeds the top of the client's $450,000-$600,000 assumption, so a written explanation to the General Counsel is required. Expert fees are the largest swing item, and the client wants quotes before engagement.
N4fc817.33. [position] Client rules on witnesses and contacts: contact Telltale employees only through the General Counsel's office and do not approach treating physicians or other providers without her approval. Plan two-hour preparation sessions with field clinical engineers and the reliability group (likely company witnesses). Tell her in advance of any corporate-representative designation and let her see the topics. Do not offer anyone from the manufacturing floor without speaking to her.
N4fc817.34. [lesson] The client wants to see any draft stipulation before it is sent. Mr. Brevik told plaintiff's counsel on March 7, 2025 that he would put the expert-report sealing term in his own draft stipulation. Clear any draft with Ms. Salvesen before it goes to plaintiff's counsel.
N4fc817.35. [lesson] Access control: all protective-order material (design review minutes, supplier file) and the mediation statement are kept in a restricted folder and must not be used in other matters or shared with other Telltale counsel. The memo proposes confirming in writing who on the firm's side has access.
N4fc817.36. [fact] Risks identified in the July 2025 memo: the 2019 manufacturing cluster and lot membership; the surgeon's testimony on sleeve technique; jury sympathy for a working man with a lasting injury; and the possibility that plaintiff uses the mediation to preview an aggressive punitive damages theory.

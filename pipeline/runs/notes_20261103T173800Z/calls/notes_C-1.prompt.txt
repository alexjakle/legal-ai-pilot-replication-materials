# DEFENDANT JOAD AXLE WORKS' ANSWER AND AFFIRMATIVE DEFENSES TO COMPLAINT FOR WRONGFUL DEATH

Defendant Joad Axle Works ("Joad") answers the Complaint filed May 30, 2023, by the Estate of Sven Lindqvist (the "Estate") as follows.

## Introduction

Joad extends its condolences to the family of Mr. Lindqvist. It denies, however, that the JX-9 tandem drive axle assembly installed on the tractor Mr. Lindqvist was driving was defectively designed or manufactured, and denies that any act or omission of Joad caused the rollover of June 9, 2022.

## Responses to Allegations

1. Joad admits that it is a manufacturer of heavy-truck axle assemblies and that it is subject to personal jurisdiction in this State. Joad denies the remaining allegations of Paragraph 1.

2. Joad lacks knowledge or information sufficient to admit or deny the allegations of Paragraphs 2 and 3 concerning the decedent's age, residence and family, and the appointment of Karin Lindqvist as personal representative, and on that basis denies them.

3. Joad admits that it manufactured a JX-9 tandem drive axle assembly that was sold in 2018 to a tractor builder and installed in a tractor later operated by Redfern Freight. Joad denies that it sold the assembly to the decedent or to Redfern Freight directly.

4. Joad lacks knowledge sufficient to admit or deny the allegations in Paragraphs 5 through 9 regarding the route, speed, load and driving conduct on June 9, 2022, including the allegation that the tractor-trailer overturned on the State Route 114 exit ramp, and therefore denies them.

5. Joad admits, on information and belief, that after the incident the drive axle's left spindle was found separated at or near the inboard bearing shoulder. Joad denies the allegation that the separation preceded and caused the loss of control, and denies that the separation was the result of a defect in design or manufacture.

6. Joad denies the allegations of Paragraphs 10 through 18 concerning design defect, including that the spindle fillet radius and heat treatment were inadequate, that Joad's fatigue testing was deficient, and that an economically feasible alternative design existed.

7. Joad denies the allegations of Paragraphs 19 through 24 concerning negligence and failure to warn, including that Joad failed to provide adequate inspection and maintenance guidance.

8. Joad lacks knowledge or information sufficient to admit or deny the allegations of Paragraphs 25 through 31 concerning the decedent's earnings, the Estate's economic losses alleged to exceed $2,400,000, and the survivors' loss of companionship, and denies them. Joad denies that the Estate is entitled to $11,500,000 or to any punitive damages.

9. Joad denies each allegation not expressly admitted.

## Affirmative and Other Defenses

Joad pleads the following defenses in the alternative without taking on any burden that the law places on the Estate.

**First Defense (Failure to State a Claim).** The Complaint fails to state a claim on which relief can be granted, in particular as to punitive damages.

**Second Defense (No Defect).** The JX-9 was reasonably safe for its intended and reasonably foreseeable uses when it left Joad's control.

**Third Defense (Industry Standards and State of the Art).** The JX-9 was designed, tested and manufactured in conformity with applicable industry standards and the state of the art in 2018.

**Fourth Defense (Substantial Change or Alteration).** The assembly was changed after it left Joad's control. Any wear, repair, re-greasing, bearing replacement, overloading or modification occurring after sale may have contributed to the separation, and Joad is not responsible for those changes.

**Fifth Defense (Inadequate Maintenance).** The assembly was subject to a recommended inspection and lubrication schedule. To the extent the tractor's owner and operator failed to follow it, their conduct was the sole or a contributing cause.

**Sixth Defense (Intervening and Superseding Cause).** The conduct of third parties, including the employer, the tractor builder and the maintenance providers, was an independent cause of the rollover.

**Seventh Defense (Comparative Fault).** To the extent the decedent drove at an excessive speed for the ramp curvature, exceeded the permitted hours of service, or failed to conduct a pre-trip inspection, the Estate's recovery is barred or reduced.

**Eighth Defense (Apportionment).** Any liability of Joad must be several only and apportioned among all persons responsible, whether or not parties to this action.

**Ninth Defense (Causation).** The condition of the spindle after the rollover may result from the rollover's impact forces rather than precede them. The Estate cannot show the separation caused the rollover.

**Tenth Defense (Adequate Warnings).** The product literature adequately warned of the need for periodic inspection of spindle and bearing components.

**Eleventh Defense (Limitations and Repose).** The claims are barred to the extent they are untimely under the applicable limitation and repose periods.

**Twelfth Defense (Setoff).** Joad is entitled to a credit for amounts paid by other persons in satisfaction of damages for the same injury.

**Thirteenth Defense (Punitive Damages).** The Complaint alleges no facts showing willful or reckless conduct, and an award would violate due process.

**Fourteenth Defense (Preservation).** The Estate must preserve the tractor, the fractured spindle, bearings, wheel-end components and maintenance records. Joad requests a joint inspection protocol before any testing.

**Fifteenth Defense (Reservation).** Joad reserves the right to amend this Answer.

WHEREFORE, Joad asks that the Complaint be dismissed, that judgment be entered in its favor, and that it be awarded its costs.

# MEMORANDUM

**To:** Cornelius Vandeleur, Partner
**From:** Imani Rutherford, Associate
**Date:** January 22, 2024
**Re:** Estate of Lindqvist v. Joad Axle Works (Tamarack County Superior Court, No. TC-23-0459) — Case Assessment and Strategy

## 1. Overview

On June 9, 2022, Sven Lindqvist, 47, was driving a Redfern Freight tractor-trailer down the State Route 114 exit ramp when the rig rolled onto its side. He died at the scene. The left spindle of the JX-9 tandem drive axle was found separated at the inboard bearing shoulder. The Estate, through Karin Lindqvist, claims the spindle fractured first, causing the loss of control, because of an inadequate fillet radius and heat treatment, and that Joad's pre-release fatigue testing was inadequate. The Estate claims economic loss above $2.4 million and demands $11.5 million.

My assessment is that this is the most serious exposure in our docket for Joad. The injury is a death, the plaintiff is sympathetic, and the physical evidence of a fractured axle part is easy for a jury to understand. On the other hand, the sequence of events, whether the spindle failed before the rollover or in it, is genuinely contested and could decide the case. I estimate a gross verdict range if liability is established of $3.5 million to $7 million, with a probability of an adverse liability finding of about 35 percent.

## 2. The Central Question: Sequence

Plaintiff's metallurgist, Dr. Fenwick Ashdown, will argue that beach marks on the fracture surface show fatigue progression over many months and that the spindle was already cracked before June 9. Our metallurgist's preliminary read is that the final overload zone is large and the fracture surface shows heavy smearing from post-separation contact, which is consistent with an impact failure. Separately, Joad's privileged post-accident metallurgical review identified a tool mark from machining at the fracture origin that the reviewer could not rule out as the initiation point. That report has not been produced and is being withheld as work product. This is the first fight in the case. If the spindle separated because of the rollover, the design-defect theory collapses.

Plaintiff's reconstruction witness, Dr. Lorraine Pettibone, will say the ramp speed was within the legal limit and that the driver had no reasonable explanation for the loss of control except an axle failure. We have a speed estimate from the tractor's electronic data recorder showing 41 miles per hour on a ramp posted at 35, which supports comparative fault and an alternative cause.

## 3. Test Data and the FT-77 Series

Joad's fatigue series FT-77, run in 2017, is the center of discovery. Four specimens in the series reached the runout; one failed at 82 percent of the runout. Plaintiff will argue that the failure shows an inadequate margin. Plaintiff may also seek to depose Hollis Pruett, a former Joad test technician who carried out the testing in the FT-77 fatigue series and left the company in 2019, about how it was conducted. The data will be produced only under the protective order, and consistent with Mr. Thorsby's direction, we will not stipulate to the authenticity of the internal test data until the order is in place. We have said so to Ms. Brandvold, and she has been reasonable, agreeing to an attorneys'-eyes-only tier. After the order is signed we can discuss authenticity document by document, and I recommend that we hold the line on any blanket stipulation, since that data will be a core part of plaintiff's affirmative case.

## 4. Strategy: Standards Defense

I recommend that we develop the industry-standards defense early and not wait for the design expert's report. The JX-9 spindle geometry and the fatigue protocol were designed to the consensus standards for heavy-vehicle axle components. To explain those to a jury, we should retain Dr. Mathilde Grosvenor, who chairs the axle subcommittee of the relevant heavy-vehicle standards body, as a witness on how the standards are written and how manufacturers are expected to satisfy them. She should be a separate engagement from our testifying design engineer, Dr. Raul Esquivel. Dr. Esquivel will address this axle's design and the testing; Dr. Grosvenor will address what the standards require. Keeping them apart avoids having one expert vouch for both the design and the yardstick used to judge it, and it shields Dr. Esquivel from cross-examination on committee matters. Dr. Grosvenor's retention letter should limit her to the standards and their history, and her work should be kept apart from the testing analysis.

Our first target, though, should be plaintiff's causation expert. Before we engage on the merits of the design-defect theory, I want to find out whether that expert's opinion that the spindle was already cracked before June 9 is reliable and fits the facts of this case. Specifically, does it rest on any testing or replication of the failure? Does it rule out the alternative explanations, including separation during the rollover and the 41 mile-per-hour recorder reading? And what does the spindle itself show, given the smearing our metallurgist has described and the eight months it sat in a yard? I would mount that challenge first. If the causation case is narrowed, the fillet radius and heat treatment arguments matter far less, and the design dispute becomes less relevant.

## 5. Settlement

Mr. Thorsby has told us that Joad's insurer, Cobaltridge Indemnity, has pre-approved settlement authority up to $1.5 million. Anything beyond that tier needs the written sign-off of Joad's board of directors. No offer above $1.5 million should be communicated, even as a signal, until we have that document in hand. The Estate has not made a formal opening demand beyond the complaint figure. I do not recommend settlement talks now. The sequence evidence is too uncertain, and the Estate will price the case on the worst version of our test data. After the spindle examination, we should revisit, and if the sequence picture is favorable, a number in the $1.2 million to $2 million range could be explored. A figure in the upper half of that range would require the board process, which takes about three weeks, so we must flag it early.

## 6. Risks

- **FT-77.** A specimen that failed short of runout.
- **Jury sympathy.** A widow with two teenage children.
- **Maintenance records.** If Redfern's last bearing service did not follow the schedule, our alteration and maintenance defenses improve, but plaintiff may try to blame Joad's manual.
- **Spoliation.** The spindle was stored for eight months in a yard before it was moved. We must establish the chain of custody.
- **Punitive claim.** The Estate pleads it but alleges no specific facts.

## 7. Next Steps and Budget

Inspection protocol and joint lab examination (February); engage Dr. Esquivel and Dr. Grosvenor; subpoena Redfern's maintenance records; prepare for Dr. Ashdown's deposition. Projected fees and costs through trial: $780,000 to $960,000.

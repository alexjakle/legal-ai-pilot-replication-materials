**DRAFT ANSWER — CIRCULATED FOR PARTNER COMMENT**

IN THE DISTRICT COURT OF PELHAM RIDGE COUNTY

NKECHI OKAFOR, Plaintiff,

v.

TOIL & TROUBLE HOME PRODUCTS, Defendant.

Cause No. D-2026-1184

# DEFENDANT'S ORIGINAL ANSWER AND AFFIRMATIVE DEFENSES

Defendant Toil & Trouble Home Products ("Toil & Trouble") files this Answer to Plaintiff's Original Petition, filed February 17, 2026, and states:

## I. General Denial

Toil & Trouble generally denies each and every allegation in the Petition, and any amendment or supplement, and demands strict proof by a preponderance of the evidence. To the extent that the applicable rules of procedure require specific responses, Toil & Trouble provides them below without waiving its general denial.

## II. Specific Responses

1. Toil & Trouble admits it designs and sells countertop electric pressure cookers, including the PressureMate TT-6200, a six-quart model. It denies that it designed, manufactured or marketed a defective product.

2. Toil & Trouble lacks sufficient information to admit or deny where Plaintiff lives or whether she purchased the unit from Marlowe Home & Hardware in October 2024 for $119.99, and therefore denies those allegations. It admits that the TT-6200 is generally sold through retail outlets.

3. Toil & Trouble admits that the TT-6200 has a lid with a rotating locking ring and a float-valve lockout pin that is designed to prevent the lid from turning while the cooker is pressurized. It denies Plaintiff's description of those components as "easily defeated."

4. Toil & Trouble denies that the unit in Plaintiff's possession was in the same condition as when it left Toil & Trouble's control. Toil & Trouble has not yet inspected the unit and demands that it be preserved in its present condition.

5. Toil & Trouble lacks knowledge sufficient to admit or deny the allegations about what occurred in Plaintiff's kitchen on December 14, 2025, including whether the lid opened while the cooker was under pressure, what Plaintiff was cooking, or how she handled the unit. Those allegations are denied.

6. Toil & Trouble lacks knowledge sufficient to admit or deny the nature or extent of Plaintiff's burn injuries, her medical treatment, her alleged expenses of $87,500, or her alleged lost income. Those allegations are denied.

7. Toil & Trouble denies the allegations of the design defect count, including that a feasible alternative design, such as a secondary mechanical lockout, existed and would have prevented the incident at a reasonable cost.

8. Toil & Trouble denies the allegations of the negligence count, including those alleging failure to test and failure to investigate prior complaints.

9. Toil & Trouble denies that Plaintiff is entitled to damages of $650,000 or to exemplary damages.

## III. Affirmative and Other Defenses

Pleading in the alternative and without assuming any burden of proof that rests on Plaintiff, Toil & Trouble asserts:

**A. Product alteration or modification.** If the unit was altered, damaged or repaired after it left Toil & Trouble's control, including by replacement of the gasket or removal of the lockout pin, those changes and not any original condition caused the incident.

**B. Misuse and failure to follow warnings.** The user manual and the product itself warn users not to force the lid, not to open the cooker until the pressure indicator has dropped, and not to overfill beyond the two-thirds line. If Plaintiff did not follow these warnings, her claims are barred or reduced.

**C. Comparative responsibility.** Plaintiff's own acts or omissions were a cause of her injuries. Toil & Trouble requests that the trier of fact assign percentages of responsibility to every person who contributed.

**D. Responsible third parties.** Toil & Trouble reserves the right to designate any retailer, prior owner or repair provider as a responsible third party once discovery permits.

**E. Compliance with standards.** The TT-6200 was designed, tested and labeled in compliance with applicable safety standards and with the safety listing it obtained from an independent laboratory before sale.

**F. Open and obvious risk.** The risk of scalding from steam and hot contents is open and obvious and is inherent in the use of a pressure cooker.

**G. Adequate warnings.** The warnings and instructions were adequate in content and placement.

**H. No safer alternative design.** No safer alternative design was economically and technologically feasible when the unit was made that would have prevented the injuries without impairing the product's utility.

**I. Lack of causation.** Nothing Toil & Trouble did or failed to do was a producing or proximate cause of the injuries alleged.

**J. Failure to mitigate.** To the extent Plaintiff failed to follow medical advice or to take reasonable steps to limit her damages, recovery is reduced.

**K. Limit on medical expenses.** Recovery of medical expenses, if any, is limited to amounts actually paid or incurred by or on behalf of Plaintiff.

**L. Exemplary damages.** The Petition pleads no facts supporting a finding of conscious indifference. Any award would be subject to statutory caps and constitutional limits.

**M. Credit and offset.** Toil & Trouble is entitled to a credit for any settlement with any other person.

**N. Preservation.** Plaintiff must preserve the cooker, lid, gasket, float valve, packaging, receipt and the food and liquids in the pot. Failure to do so entitles Toil & Trouble to an adverse inference.

## IV. Prayer

Toil & Trouble asks that Plaintiff take nothing, that judgment be entered for Toil & Trouble, and that it recover costs and other relief to which it is entitled.

# MEMORANDUM

**To:** Josephine Armitage, Partner
**From:** Marcus Teller, Associate
**Date:** May 12, 2026
**Re:** Okafor v. Toil & Trouble Home Products (Pelham Ridge County District Court, No. D-2026-1184) — Case Assessment

## 1. Assessment in Brief

Ms. Okafor, 38, suffered second- and third-degree scald burns to her left forearm and upper chest on December 14, 2025, when the lid of her PressureMate TT-6200 reportedly rotated open while the cooker was under pressure. She claims a design defect (the lid can be turned while the pot is pressurized) and negligence. Her medical charges are $87,500, she has had two skin graft procedures, and she is claiming scarring and about $38,000 in lost income from her work as a dental hygienist. Her demand is $650,000.

I place the likely range of exposure at $140,000 to $320,000 on the current record, with a modest chance of a defense verdict. Adverse liability is not likely to be a landslide, but a scarred, sympathetic plaintiff and a consumer product with a pressure hazard is a hard combination in front of a jury. The unit has not been inspected, so this is a preliminary view.

## 2. Key Facts

The TT-6200 has a rotating bayonet lid and a spring-loaded float valve with a lockout pin that rises as pressure builds and blocks the ring from turning. Operating pressure is about 10 psi. Ms. Okafor bought the unit on October 11, 2024. She says she was cooking dried beans, that the pressure indicator was down, and that the lid turned with ordinary effort and released a jet of hot liquid. Our client's service records show a replacement gasket was sent to a household at her address in July 2025, which raises an alteration question. A retail employee has also told the client's investigator that the box for this unit had been returned once and resold as new, which, if true, could undercut her account that she bought it new. The Q3-2024 lot has no field complaints about the lid, but there were three complaints of "lid hard to open" on other lots in 2024. Engineering change notice ECN-6200-14 added a retention clip to the lockout pin on later production and is dated after the plaintiff's purchase. Because it predates the plaintiff's injury, the subsequent-remedial-measure rule is unlikely to keep it out, and I want to settle how we treat it before the design file goes out.

## 3. Plaintiff's Likely Experts

Dr. Gareth Pellam, a mechanical engineer, will probably say that the lockout pin can stick when it is coated with starch or foam from beans, allowing the ring to turn despite the pressure. A human-factors witness, Dr. Yvette Hargrove, will address the manual's warnings. We should assume both will be designated.

## 4. Strategy

**Order of attack.** The first target is Dr. Pellam's opinion on what actually happened in this kitchen. Before we engage on whether a different lid mechanism would be a better design, we want to find out whether his testimony that this unit failed from a sticking pin is based on testing and fits the facts: the ordinary-use scenario Ms. Okafor describes, the replacement gasket, and the condition of the pin when the unit is examined. If he has not replicated the failure on a comparable unit, or has not ruled out a gasket problem, his reliability is open to challenge, and that challenge should be mounted before we spend effort on the merits of the design theory. A narrowed causation case makes the alternative design argument less relevant.

**Mediation first.** Toil & Trouble wants mediation to come before expert discovery, and we negotiated that into the schedule: mediation by July 17, 2026, plaintiff's designations August 21 and ours October 2. I recommend retired trial judge Harlan Whitcombe as mediator. For mediation to be useful, we need the inspection finished by the end of June and our engineer's preliminary read in hand, but not a formal report. That keeps expert spending low until we see whether the case resolves.

**Inspection.** Dr. Oswin Takahashi, our engineer, should attend the joint inspection. The key facts to preserve are the pin's travel, residue on the pin and seat, the gasket's origin and the state of the ring.

## 5. Reporting to the Client

Mr. Oyelaran's letter says he wants case reports in a one-page format with an exposure range, not narrative memos. This memo is for internal use; the client report I will draft after the inspection will be a single page with a range, a short status line, and the next event. My current number for that page is $140,000 to $320,000, with $230,000 as a midpoint.

## 6. Risks

1. **Sticking-pin evidence.** If the unit's pin shows residue consistent with bean foam, Dr. Pellam's theory will be much stronger.
2. **Complaint history.** Three "hard to open" complaints will be characterized as notice.
3. **The replacement gasket.** It cuts both ways: it supports alteration, but if we sent the part, plaintiff will say we sent the wrong one.
4. **Damages.** Two grafts and visible scarring.
5. **Spoliation.** We must confirm the unit has been preserved since December. Ms. Trevisan says it has been in a lab since February.

## 7. Next Steps

- Send the inspection protocol (done April 24).
- Ask Dr. Takahashi for availability the last two weeks of June.
- Confirm with Mr. Oyelaran that Mr. Whitcombe is acceptable and obtain preliminary authority for the mediation session.
- Pull the July 2025 gasket shipment records.
- Prepare a first one-page client report for the file by May 29.

## 8. Budget

Through mediation: $52,000 to $68,000. Through trial if the case does not resolve: $240,000 to $310,000.

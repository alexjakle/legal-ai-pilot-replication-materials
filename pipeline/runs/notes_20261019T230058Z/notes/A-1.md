# Notes: A-1 (Telltale Cardiac Systems)

N412193.01. [fact] Matter A-1: Hartwell v. Telltale Cardiac Systems, Ashgrove County Superior Court, Case No. 25-CV-03817. Plaintiff Dorothea Hartwell filed her complaint on March 4, 2025. Kenge, Carboy & Vholes LLP defends Telltale Cardiac Systems; Walter Osgood is the partner and Priya Raghunathan the day-to-day associate. Plaintiff's counsel is Gideon Farrar.
N412193.02. [fact] Plaintiff alleges that the conductor coil of the ArcLine AL-4120 bipolar active-fixation pacing lead was unreasonably prone to fatigue fracture, that a safer alternative design was feasible, that Telltale knew of a heightened fracture rate before her implant, and that Telltale failed to warn implanting physicians of fracture risk near the clavicle and first rib. She demands compensatory damages above $1.9 million plus punitive damages.
N412193.03. [fact] The AL-4120 was implanted in Ms. Hartwell on March 19, 2019, at Westmarch Regional Hospital, through a subclavian approach. A routine interrogation on November 8, 2022, showed out-of-range impedance. The lead was revised surgically on November 15, 2022. Radiographs show the break about 4 cm from the pectoral pocket, in the segment that crosses beneath the clavicle.
N412193.04. [fact] Claimed damages: $214,000 in billed charges for the revision surgery and roughly $96,000 in lost earnings (Ms. Hartwell is a school administrator), plus general damages. The associate's memo of October 7, 2025 estimates realistic verdict exposure of $450,000 to $900,000 if liability is found, and fees and costs through trial of $410,000 to $520,000.
N412193.05. [fact] Telltale's complaint database shows 31 confirmed conductor fractures among about 22,000 AL-4120 leads shipped, a rate near 0.14 percent. The memo says engineering must still confirm that the rate stayed within the labeled range.
N412193.06. [fact] An engineer in the field circulated an internal note in 2020 saying returned leads showed wear from repeated bending near the pocket and suggesting the design be revisited. As of the October 2025 memo it had not been produced. The associate regards it as the most damaging undisclosed document.
N412193.07. [fact] A 2021 trend report followed an internal Telltale quality inquiry into returned-lead fractures, called the Larkspur review. As of the October 2025 memo, plaintiff had not asked about Larkspur by name. Plaintiff's regulatory witness is expected to say Telltale should have updated its warnings after the 2021 trend report.
N412193.08. [fact] The AL-4120 instructions for use warn against medial subclavian puncture and describe lateral venous access as the preferred approach. The operative note does not say which technique was used, and the memo calls that the most important open factual question.
N412193.09. [fact] Plaintiff's expected experts are Dr. Ruth Kellaway (biomedical engineer, who will say the coil pitch and insulation sleeve create a stress concentration), Dr. Leopold Quist (electrophysiologist, on causation), and a regulatory witness. Telltale's testifying design engineer is Dr. Henrik Vandermeer. As of the memo, Dr. Kellaway had not examined the explanted lead under magnification and had not ruled out subclavian crush from medial puncture.
N412193.10. [fact] The draft Answer, dated April 22, 2025 and marked as not yet filed and for partner review, admits that Telltale makes cardiac leads, that the AL-4120 was implanted on or about March 19, 2019, and (on information and belief) that the interrogation and revision occurred on November 8 and 15, 2022. It denies defect, inadequate warnings, and causation. It pleads lack of knowledge on plaintiff's residence, age, and damages.
N412193.11. [position] Telltale's draft Answer pleads fifteen defenses. They include failure to state a claim, learned intermediary, adequate warnings, state of the art, regulatory compliance and preemption, no feasible safer alternative, causation, superseding cause, comparative fault, failure to mitigate, limitations and repose, misuse or alteration, setoff, and constitutional limits on punitive damages. The Answer also reserves the right to add defenses.
N412193.12. [position] Learned intermediary defense: Telltale contends its duty to warn ran to the implanting physician, who was adequately warned through the instructions for use and related communications, and who exercised independent medical judgment. The memo says Dr. Anselm Teague's testimony drives both this defense and the causation challenge.
N412193.13. [fact] The draft Answer's own drafting notes flag three open points for the partner: whether to plead repose given the 2019 implant date, whether the client has signed off on the preemption language, and that the Eleventh Defense (limitations/repose) is a placeholder pending plaintiff's chart.
N412193.14. [position] The firm's associate (October 2025 memo) recommends dropping the statute of repose defense from the final Answer because it is likely unavailable given the March 2019 implant date.
N412193.15. [fact] Discovery scope agreed by email in June 2025: Telltale will produce the AL-4120 design history file and the testing that supported its release. Complaint and returned-product analysis records for the AL-4120 run from first clinical use in 2018 through the November 15, 2022 revision date.
N412193.16. [position] Telltale refuses to produce design files for the whole ArcLine family. It argues that models differ in coil pitch and insulation thickness and that a family-wide request is disproportionate in a single-model case. Mr. Farrar counters that Telltale's labeling treats ArcLine as one platform. He will consider limiting the request to the AL-4120 and its closest sibling only if given an engineering declaration on the differences.
N412193.17. [position] Telltale agreed to send an informal engineering declaration limited to coil differences between the AL-4120 and the AL-4125, on the condition that it is not treated as a concession on family-wide relevance. The memo expects that declaration to hold the line on those two models against continued pressure for sibling-model files.
N412193.18. [position] Telltale refused to produce post-revision events for leads returned from the implanting center, on the grounds that the request goes beyond Telltale's knowledge of its own product and implicates third-party patient information. Mr. Farrar had conditioned his agreement on the complaint window on receiving those events. The email thread does not record that dispute being resolved, and Ms. Raghunathan asked him to say if he intends to move on it.
N412193.19. [fact] Expert schedule agreed by email on June 19, 2025, subject to court approval of a stipulation: plaintiff's opening reports are due January 16, 2026, and Telltale's reports are due March 17, 2026. The parties agreed to sequential, not simultaneous, disclosure.
N412193.20. [fact] Counsel agreed that consulting experts are not discoverable and that draft expert reports and attorney–testifying-expert communications are protected, except for facts, data and assumptions counsel provided. Mr. Farrar was to send a stipulation, and Telltale was to circulate a draft protective order covering design and testing documents.
N412193.21. [fact] Dr. Anselm Teague, the treating cardiologist, was first noticed for deposition for the week of September 15, 2025. It slipped and was reset to November 6, 2025. Mr. Farrar refused to defer all expert depositions until after Dr. Teague testifies, citing uncertainty about his availability; Ms. Raghunathan said the expert deposition sequence would be revisited if he could not sit in the original window.
N412193.22. [fact] Dr. Teague is the only witness who can say whether lateral or medial access was used and what he understood from the instructions for use.
N412193.23. [position] Strategy (memo): direct the first motion practice and first expert effort at Dr. Quist's and Dr. Kellaway's causation opinions rather than the design-defect debate. The aim is to test whether their opinion that the lead failed from design rather than placement is reliable and fits the facts. If a ruling limits Dr. Kellaway's causation opinion, the design theory loses its link to the injury. Only if that fails does Dr. Vandermeer meet the design theory directly. The associate rates summary judgment prospects as modest and trial prospects as good if causation is narrowed first.
N412193.24. [position] Client instruction: Telltale will make no settlement offer until Ms. Hartwell's treating cardiologist has been deposed and the transcript read. Counsel must not engage on figures or signal willingness to pay before then. Afterward the firm sends the General Counsel an assessment and recommended range. Authority above $500,000 needs sign-off from the CFO, Cormac Brightwater.
N412193.25. [position] Settlement posture (memo): Ms. Raghunathan has told plaintiff's counsel only that the firm cannot discuss numbers. Mr. Farrar has asked twice for a mediation date. The firm recommends declining until after the November 6, 2025 deposition and, if the transcript is favorable, proposing mediation in January 2026, with an authority request only after the deposition.
N412193.26. [fact] Telltale's product liability program carries a $750,000 self-insured retention per claim. Thaddeus Wren, the outside insurance liaison, coordinates with the excess carrier, and coverage positions may be discussed only with him or the General Counsel.
N412193.27. [position] Client instruction on experts: Telltale wants to be consulted before any testifying expert is engaged, with three names per discipline and a note on prior testimony. Its regulatory affairs group, led by Odalys Fennimore, must review every expert report before service, allowing ten business days, to check that nothing conflicts with what Telltale told the regulator. Reports may not go outside the firm and the experts until cleared.
N412193.28. [position] Firm plan for regulatory review (memo): Dr. Vandermeer to deliver a near-final draft by January 30, 2026, ahead of the March 17, 2026 deadline. Comments from the regulatory group must go through counsel, and what is sent must be logged so the exchange stays protected as work done at counsel's direction. The firm will confirm with Mr. Farrar that the draft-protection stipulation covers this process.
N412193.29. [position] Industry-standards defense (memo): begin developing it now rather than waiting for Dr. Kellaway's reports, with a witness from the cardiac-device standards community explaining how standards for a lead like the AL-4120 are written and satisfied. That witness should be engaged separately from Dr. Vandermeer. Otherwise plaintiff could argue he is grading his own work, and a separate witness keeps him out of standards-committee questions.
N412193.30. [fact] Client reporting and handling requirements: a monthly written update and a call after significant events; at least five business days' notice before filing any motion; phase budgets with variance explained above ten percent; all communications to the General Counsel with a copy to Mr. Wren; no more than two timekeepers per deposition at Telltale's expense; and a biweekly call with Ms. Raghunathan in the early phase.
N412193.31. [fact] Client budget and sensitivities: Telltale's working assumption is that the case should be resolved or tried for under $500,000 in fees and costs combined, and it wants an explanation if the estimate goes higher. The memo's estimate of $410,000 to $520,000 straddles that figure. Because Telltale is in a regulatory inspection tied to its next-generation lead, it asks the firm to avoid public filings that characterize failure rates beyond what the case requires.
N412193.32. [fact] Client confidentiality and preservation instructions: contact with the media, treating physicians, or employees below vice-president level needs prior clearance, apart from the interviews the client set up. All AL-4120 and ArcLine-family documents must be preserved under a litigation hold. Design engineering and complaint-handling records (tracked in the Orrinwick database) are confidential, and a protective order must be proposed before producing them.
N412193.33. [position] Telltale's overall view is that the AL-4120 was not defective, citing a long field record and its own complaint data. It wants a vigorous but measured defense.
N412193.34. [fact] Risks identified in the memo: (1) the missing operative detail on access technique, since medial puncture strengthens the defense and lateral access strengthens plaintiff's inherent-fatigue theory; (2) plaintiff arguing the 2021 trend report shows notice of a rising fracture rate; (3) Ms. Hartwell's credibility and sympathy after a hard recovery; (4) discovery creep on sibling-model files; (5) the likely unavailability of repose.
N412193.35. [fact] Next steps listed in the October 2025 memo: finalize the Dr. Teague deposition outline; get Dr. Vandermeer's CV and availability; outline a motion to exclude Dr. Quist's and Dr. Kellaway's opinions; and send Ms. Salvesen the November status note.

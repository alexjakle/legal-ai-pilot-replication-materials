## 2. Realism problems

### Legal and procedural

- **Preemption pleaded on a "cleared" device (A-1/01:31, :57).** The answer says the AL-4120 was "cleared for distribution following the federal regulator's review of Telltale's premarket submission", which is 510(k) language. It then pleads federal preemption. Clearance generally supports no express preemption, and implantable pacing leads are Class III PMA devices in the real world. A-2 says "approved" for a sibling lead from the same family (A-2/01:21, :47). The answers use inconsistent regulatory terms for the same product line.
- **Subsequent remedial measure misanalysed (B-1/03:18).** An ECN "dated after the plaintiff's purchase" is not a subsequent remedial measure. Rule 407 (and its state analogues) is keyed to the injury or event, which here is Dec 14, 2025, not the purchase. Rule 407 is also an admissibility rule, not a ground for delaying production of the design file, which the memo wants "settled before the design file goes out" and which counsel has agreed to produce by June 5 (B-1/02:65).
- **Withholding known responsive documents (A-1/03:16, A-2/03:20, C-1/03:18).** Each memo describes a damaging document within agreed scope and treats non-production as a status, not a problem. A skeptical reviewer would expect a privilege-log or production analysis. None appears. C-1 asserts work product over a company "post-accident metallurgical review" without saying who commissioned it or when.
- **Client regulatory group pre-clearing testifying expert reports (A-1/04:28, A-1/03:32, A-2/04:30, A-2/03:36).** Having the client's regulatory affairs group review and comment on testifying expert reports before service creates a serious independence problem and a discoverability risk. Comments that shape opinions are likely "facts, data, or assumptions" provided by counsel. The memo's fix ("comments from the regulatory group go through counsel") is thin, and no one flags the cross-examination exposure.
- **Answer denials inconsistent with known facts (A-2/01:25 vs A-2/03:20).** The answer "specifically denies ... that Telltale knew of a pattern of such failures before February 2020". The later memo describes a 2019 cluster of eleven returns and a 2019 undisclosed supplier formula change, and nobody revisits the denial.
- **Denial without knowledge (B-1/01:29).** "Toil & Trouble denies that the unit ... was in the same condition as when it left Toil & Trouble's control." The next sentence admits it has not inspected the unit. This should be a lack-of-knowledge response.
- **"Preservation" pleaded as an affirmative defense (B-1/01:71 Defense N; C-1/01:71 Fourteenth Defense).** A preservation demand is not a defense, and asserting an entitlement to an adverse inference in an answer is not how that works.
- **"demands strict proof by a preponderance of the evidence" (B-1/01:19).** This mixes old common-law "strict proof" boilerplate with a standard of proof. Texas-style general denials do not say this.
- **"collision" for a single-vehicle rollover (C-1/01:19, :55).** The answer calls the event a "collision" and blames "other motorists". The answer itself (line 29) and the memo describe a rollover on an exit ramp, with no other vehicle mentioned anywhere.
- **Statute of limitations / repose boilerplate on facts that defeat it.** C-1: death June 9, 2022, complaint May 30, 2023, sale 2018 (C-1/01:65). Limitations is plainly not available and repose almost certainly not. A-1 at least flags this (A-1/01:91, A-1/03:42).
- **Answer timing (A-1).** The complaint was filed Mar 4, 2025 and the answer draft is dated Apr 22, 2025 (49 days). That is possible with service lag or an extension, but neither is mentioned.
- **Late "initial" case assessments.** A-1/04:33 requires an assessment within 45 days of Mar 27, 2025, but the memo titled "Case Assessment and Strategy" is dated Oct 7, 2025. For A-2, the deadline is 60 days from Sept 20, 2024 (A-2/04:36), but the memo is dated July 18, 2025. For C-1, the deadline is 60 days from June 22, 2023 (C-1/04:35), but the memo is dated Jan 22, 2024. None of the memos acknowledges an earlier assessment.
- **Expert sequencing rationale reversed (A-1/02:35).** "a simultaneous exchange favors the party with the burden of proof". The usual argument is the opposite: defendants want sequential disclosure so they can respond to the plaintiff's opinions. The conclusion is right but the reason given is odd.
- **Garbled expert schedule (A-2/02:35).** "simultaneous opening reports on September 29 are workable for plaintiff's affirmative case, but we want a rebuttal round. I propose October 31, 2025 for defense reports". That is sequential disclosure, not simultaneous. The email contradicts itself.
- **A-2/02:29.** "once a protective order is in place, which I understand the Court entered on November 12, 2024". Counsel of record who moved for the order (as instructed at A-2/04:22) would not hedge "which I understand". Lowenthal had already cited the date in the previous email.
- **A-2/03:18.** "Our design expert ... believes the sleeve instructions were followed in the design validation". This is incoherent: the question is whether the implanting surgeon followed the sleeve instructions, not the validation program.
- **A-2/03:36.** "a complete draft by September 26, which leaves about two weeks for the review". Sept 26 to the Oct 31 deadline is 35 days. The arithmetic does not match.
- **B-1/03:34.** "$140,000 to $320,000, with $225,000 as a midpoint". The midpoint is $230,000.
- **C-1/02:33.** "defense reports to follow plaintiff's by four months, so July 3, 2024". Four months from Mar 8 is July 8. (Line 50's "roughly 98 days" is correct.)
- **C-1/03:34.** "A figure in the upper half of that range would require the board process". The board threshold is $1.5M (C-1/04:26), which falls in the lower half of $1.2M–$2.0M (midpoint $1.6M). Figures from $1.5M to $1.6M also need the board.
- **Authenticity stipulation tied to the protective order (C-1/04:22, C-1/02:31, C-1/03:24).** Stipulating that a document is authentic discloses nothing, so linking authenticity to confidentiality makes little sense. It is internally consistent across all three documents, but a real GC and associate would separate the two.

### Internal and cross-document inconsistencies within a matter or client

- **Telltale insurance retention.** A-1/04:56 says "self-insured retention of $750,000 per claim". A-2/04:62 says "Our retention is $1,200,000 per claim". This is the same GC and the same product liability program, six months apart, and neither letter mentions a change.
- **A-1 field record.** A-1/04:18 says "The AL-4120 has a long field record". The lead was released in 2018 (A-1/02:12) and the letter is from 2025.
- **A-1 regulatory review timing.** A-1/03:32 attributes the ten-day review to "Ms. Salvesen's team". The letter assigns it to Odalys Fennimore's regulatory group.
- **A-1 and A-2 run in parallel at the same firm, for the same client and product family, with different partners.** Neither file acknowledges the other. A-2's instruction against sharing strategy "with other counsel for Telltale" (A-2/04:46) would cover the A-1 team.
- **B-1 gasket.** The letter (B-1/04:48) records "one contact ... about a replacement gasket". The memo (B-1/03:18) hardens this to "a replacement gasket was sent". It is a small escalation with no stated source.
- **C-1.** The memo (C-1/03:24) says the technician Pruett "carried out the testing". The letter (C-1/04:51) says Oakhurst "ran the 2017 test program". That is reconcilable but never reconciled.

### Machine-generated tells

- **Systematic avoidance of real-world proper nouns.** The documents say "the federal regulator" (never FDA), "the voluntary consensus standard for implantable pacing leads" (never named), "the relevant heavy-vehicle standards body", "an independent laboratory" and "this State". No state is ever named. There are no statutes, rules or case citations anywhere in 16 litigation documents.
- **No real formalities.** The answers have no bar numbers, firm address, signature lines, certificate of service or jury demand. No entity suffix appears on any party (no "Inc."/"LLC"), and there is no state of incorporation. Email domains are `.example`.
- **Literary naming.** Kenge, Carboy and Vholes are Bleak House solicitors. Other names are puns: "Telltale Cardiac" (Poe, heart), "Toil & Trouble" pressure cooker (Macbeth cauldron), "Joad" axle/truck (The Grapes of Wrath). Throughout there is a density of ornate invented names (Cormac Brightwater, Odalys Fennimore, Thaddeus Wren, Perpetua Wallace, Evander Thorsby, Constance Aldrin).
- **Uniform templating.** Every matter has exactly the same four document types, with near-identical headings ("Bottom Line/Summary", "Risks", "Budget"). Every email thread is 6 messages long, about the same topic ("Discovery Scope ... and Expert Disclosure"). Each thread follows the same arc (plaintiff overreaches on product family, defense limits to one model, plaintiff adds one sibling, defense concedes with a non-admission caveat), and each ends with the associate copying the partner. Every answer has 14–15 defenses in the same order.
- **Prose register.** The prose is uniformly clean, has no typos, and uses identical phrasing for the same ideas across authors (see §3). There is no shorthand, no internal cross-references to Bates numbers, and no docket entries.

## 5. What I could not assess

- **Governing law.** No state is named, so I could not check limitations and repose periods, answer deadlines, pleading requirements, preemption doctrine, expert-disclosure rules or SRM rules against real law. B-1 is identifiable as Texas-style only from its terminology.
- **Underlying pleadings and exhibits.** There are no complaints, protective orders, stipulations, scheduling orders, IFUs, test reports or device history records. I could not verify the paragraph-number mappings in the answers or whether the quoted allegations exist.
- **Technical accuracy.** I could not verify lead construction (whether a silicone outer over polyurethane inner layer is plausible for an AL-4135-type lead), the fracture rate base rates, pressure-cooker lockout design, or axle spindle metallurgy beyond surface plausibility.
- **Whether withheld documents are actually privileged or responsive** under the actual requests served. The requests themselves are not in the file.
- **Whether earlier client assessments exist** that would explain the late "Case Assessment" memos.
- **Metadata.** The files are Markdown with no authorship, revision or creation metadata. Per instructions I did not inspect anything outside the folder or run git.

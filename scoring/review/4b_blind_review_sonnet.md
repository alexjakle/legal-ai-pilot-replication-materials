# Review of 16 documents, Kenge, Carboy & Vholes LLP

Paths are relative to the firm folder. Documents are cited as A1 = A/A-1, A2 = A/A-2, B1 = B/B-1, C1 = C/C-1, with the file number after the slash (01 answer, 02 emails, 03 memo, 04 instruction letter). I read all 16 in full.

## 1. Out of place for the document it sits in

### A1 (Hartwell / Telltale)

- **High.** A1/03 line 20: "The trend report itself followed an internal quality inquiry that Telltale ran earlier in 2021 into returned-lead fractures, the Larkspur review, and plaintiff has not asked about it by name."
  - It is a named, undisclosed internal document dropped into a paragraph about plaintiff's experts.
  - The point of the sentence is that the review is responsive to the requests but not yet named, which no memo would say so casually.
  - The memo gives no production, privilege-log or ethics treatment for it.
- **High.** A1/03 line 34, the "Industry-standards defense" paragraph.
  - It sits under "Experts and Regulatory Review", not under Strategy, and it is bolded like a new topic.
  - It argues for a standards witness separate from the design engineer so he is not "grading his own work", and so a separate witness "keeps him out of questions about standards-committee matters".
  - Nothing else in the file says the design engineer has any committee connection. The reference dangles.
  - It also anticipates a defense that the answer pleads only generically (Fourth Defense, 01 line 55).
- **Medium.** A1/03 line 16: "In 2020 an engineer working in the field circulated an internal note observing that returned leads showed wear from repeated bending close to the pocket and suggesting the design be looked at again. It has not been produced, and I regard it as the most damaging document that has not yet been disclosed."
  - It is an unusually specific hot-document narrative in a "Facts" paragraph.
  - It has no source, no custodian, and no plan to produce or log it.
- **Medium.** A1/04 line 18: "Complaint files and returns are tracked in Orrinwick, our complaint-handling database…" and, in the same paragraph, "The company is also in the middle of a regulatory inspection tied to our next-generation lead".
  - The invented system name is an oddly precise detail in a general paragraph.

- **Medium.** A1/04 line 28 (reports must clear the "regulatory affairs group, led by Odalys Fennimore") and A1/03 line 32.
  - The letter says reports must not go "to anyone outside your firm and the experts" until cleared, yet the group is outside the firm and the experts.

- **Medium.** A1/02 line 71: "One housekeeping point. ## 2. Realism problems

### Across the pleadings (A1/01, A2/01, B1/01, C1/01)

- **No real regulator or standards body is named in any file.**
  - The text says "the federal regulator", "the relevant heavy-vehicle standards body", "the consensus standard" and similar.
  - Real answers and memos would name the agency and the standard.
  - The same avoidance in all four matters reads as abstraction by a generator.
- **Signature blocks are implausible for a filed pleading.**
  - Each reads "Associate" and "Partner", sometimes with "Reviewed:" (A1 line 88), with no bar numbers, address or certificate of service.
  - None includes a jury demand.
- **The defense lists are near-identical in all four answers.**
  - A1, A2 and C1 each have exactly 15 defenses ending in a "Reservation" defense, and B1 has 14.
  - All four include "punitive damages … due process" and "setoff".
- **Several "defenses" are not defenses.**
  - B1/01 "N. Preservation" and C1/01 "Fourteenth Defense (Preservation)" are demands, not defenses.
  - C1/01 "Ninth Defense (Causation)" and "Second Defense (No Defect)" are denials.
- **The answer paragraph numbers do not follow the complaint's.**
  - A1/01 has ten numbered paragraphs responding to complaint paragraphs 1–22 (for example answer ¶7 responds to "Paragraphs 7 through 12", and ¶9 to "18 through 22").
  - B1/01 and C1/01 do the same.
  - A real answer tracks the complaint's own paragraph numbers.
- **A1/01 line 31 says the lead was "cleared for distribution following the federal regulator's review of Telltale's premarket submission".** An implantable pacing lead is typically a PMA ("approved") device, not "cleared". The same answer's Fifth Defense pleads preemption (line 57), which does not fit a cleared device. A2/01 says "approved" (Defense 5) and "completed its review" (line 21). The two Telltale answers differ in how they describe the same regulatory path.
- **B1/01 mixes a Texas-style general denial with paragraph-by-paragraph responses.** It also demands "strict proof by a preponderance of the evidence" (line 19). The pleading otherwise looks like Texas practice (Original Answer, responsible third parties, exemplary damages, "producing cause"), where a general denial is the norm.

### A1 (Hartwell)

- **Timeline inconsistency between the answer and the memo.**
  - The answer is dated April 22, 2025, and marked "DRAFT… NOT YET FILED".
  - The June 2025 emails treat the case as in discovery.
  - The October 7, 2025 memo says to "drop [repose] from the final answer" (03 line 42).
  - By October the answer would long since have been filed.
- **A1/02 line 33 contradicts its own logic.**
  - Priya says anything after the revision date "is not relevant to what Telltale knew when the lead was implanted", but the implant was March 2019.
  - On that reasoning complaint records through November 2022 are also not relevant, and she agrees to produce them.
- **A1/02 line 35: "a simultaneous exchange favors the party with the burden of proof".** This is a weak or backwards rationale for preferring sequential reports.
- **A1/03 §6 item 4 and §2 say the AL-4125 engineering declaration "should hold the line"**, yet the email (line 67) says it will be sent "informal… not a concession". The memo gives no disclosure assessment of that declaration.
- **A1/03 treats the design engineer as retained and then not.** Line 24 says "Dr. Henrik Vandermeer, our testifying design engineer", but §7 says to "ask Dr. Vandermeer for his CV and availability". The client letter wanted three names per discipline before any engagement (04 line 28).
- **A1/04 asked for an assessment "within 45 days" (by about May 11, 2025).** The only assessment in the file is dated October 7, 2025.

### A2 (Delmore)

- **A2/02 line 35 calls simultaneous reports "workable" and then proposes defense reports October 31 and rebuttals December 5.** That is not simultaneous, and it is inconsistent within the same email.
- **A2/03 line 36:** "I have asked Dr. Ouellette for a complete draft by September 26, which leaves about two weeks for the review." September 26 to the October 31 report date is five weeks.
- **A2/03 line 18:** "Dr. Bertrand Ouellette, believes the sleeve instructions were followed in the design validation." The sentence is vague and non-technical for an engineering opinion.
- **A2/04 line 18, "mediator", and A2/03 §5:** the letter says the mediator should not learn Telltale's settlement range, but no mediator exists on that date.
- **A2/04 line 26 mixes "he or she" for the treating cardiologist.** By then the doctor and the complaint (with her identity) would be known. It is a minor tell.
- **The privilege and protective-order machinery is confused.** The memo header marks the privileged memo "SUBJECT TO PROTECTIVE ORDER" (03 line 1), and the memo treats the mediation statement as covered by that order (03 line 32).

### B1 (Okafor)

- **B1/03 line 34: "$140,000 to $320,000, with $225,000 as a midpoint."** The midpoint is $230,000.
- **B1/03 line 28 says "I recommend retired trial judge Harlan Whitcombe".** Plaintiff's counsel proposed him (B1/02 line 85), and the memo's own Next Steps (line 48) say to "Confirm… Mr. Whitcombe is acceptable". The email promised an answer "by Monday" (May 4), and nothing records it by May 12.
- **B1/04 line 28 asks for a one-page report with no narrative memos, but the first report is only due "by the end of May" (line 30)**, nearly three months into the case, and the memo is dated May 12.
- **B1/04 line 52 and B1/03 line 54:** the client's "$150,000 through mediation" ceiling sits against the memo's $52,000–$68,000. Neither side comments on the gap.
- **Draft answer dated March 31, 2026 for a petition filed February 17, 2026.** The date is late for a Texas-style answer unless service was delayed. The file says nothing about service.
- **B1/02 lines 69 and 82:** defense counsel asks plaintiff to "disclose consulting experts who inspect the unit". Consulting experts are normally protected, and a defense lawyer would not ask for their disclosure.

### C1 (Lindqvist)

- **Inspection timing is inconsistent.**
  - The October 2023 emails set a joint inspection for "the week of November 13".
  - The January 22, 2024 memo lists "Inspection protocol and joint lab examination (February)" as a future step and says "After the spindle examination, we should revisit" (03 line 34).
  - Nothing explains the slip.
- **C1/03 line 46 lists "engage Dr. Esquivel and Dr. Grosvenor" as future steps.** Line 28 calls Esquivel "our testifying design engineer", and line 18 refers to "our metallurgist's preliminary read" with no name.
- **C1/03 line 41 says the spindle sat "for eight months in a yard".** The letter says the spindle and tractor are "in a storage facility not under our control" (04 line 43), and the emails refer to a "storage facility" (02 line 65).
- **C1/03 line 14: "the most serious exposure in our docket for Joad".** A single-matter memo speaks of a docket for the client, which implies other Joad matters. The letter mentions "two other open claims" (04 line 59), but nothing else in the memo refers to them.
- **C1/04 line 59: a $400,000 retention.** The text then says the insurer "has pre-approved authority up to $1,500,000" (line 26), which blurs insurer, retention and board authority.
- **C1/02 line 33:** Imani asks for defense reports "four months" after March 8, 2024 and then gives "July 3, 2024". Four months is July 8.
- **C1/03 line 18:** the "privileged post-accident metallurgical review" is called work product. A company's own post-accident review, done before suit was filed, is a doubtful basis for that label, and the tool-mark finding is a fact.
- **C1/01 line 29, ¶4, and the Seventh Defense:** the answer pleads "excessive speed", "hours of service" and "pre-trip inspection" against the decedent with no stated basis. It also denies knowledge of the "speed", yet the memo already has the recorder reading of 41 mph in a 35 zone (03 line 20).

### Machine-generated feel (all files)

- **All four instruction letters follow one template.**
  - Sections run: The Matter/Background, Settlement or Experts, Reporting (bullets), Standing/General Guidelines (6–7 numbered items), Witnesses, Budget, Insurance.
  - The guidelines repeat in every letter: send all communications to the GC with an insurance contact copied; no media; litigation hold; a staffing limit of two or three; monthly invoices; "Please confirm receipt".
  - Four different companies would not write four letters with the same architecture.
- **None of the four letters carries a privilege legend.** The memos do.
- **Letters have no city, state or ZIP in the letterhead** (for example "1180 Quillon Parkway, Suite 400").
- **The memos and email threads are uniform.**
  - Memo files run 5.6–7.1 KB and share a skeleton (overview, key facts, plaintiff's experts, strategy, risks, budget).
  - Each email thread has five to seven messages about discovery scope and expert dates.
- **The memos keep restating the client's letter to the partner who already has it.** Examples: B1/03 line 34 ("Mr. Oyelaran's letter says…"), C1/03 line 24 ("consistent with Mr. Thorsby's direction"), A2/03 line 47, A1/03 line 28 ("Ms. Salvesen made clear in her instruction letter…").
- **The names are unusually literary and tidy:**
  - Firm: Kenge, Carboy & Vholes (all three are Bleak House solicitors).
  - Clients: Telltale Cardiac Systems, Toil & Trouble Home Products, Joad Axle Works.
  - Internal systems: Orrinwick, Ravenscar, Corbelmark.
  - Place names: Ashgrove, Linnet, Pelham Ridge, Tamarack, Quillon Parkway, Cinderfield Road.
  - All email domains are `.example`.
- **The email threads are courteous and complete.** Each has no stray typo, no missed attachment and no unanswered question, and each ends in a "confirmed" message.

## 3. Cross-matter oddities

- **The standards-witness paragraph appears in three matters with near-identical logic.**
  - Files: A1/03 line 34, A2/03 line 24, C1/03 line 28.
  - Each says to build the industry-standards defense "now" or "early", not wait for the report.
  - Each retains a separate standards witness from the design engineer.
  - Each gives the reason that one expert should not vouch for both design and yardstick, or "grading his own work".
  - Each says it shields the design engineer from cross-examination on "committee matters" or "committee politics".
  - Each tells counsel to limit the witness's scope to the standard and its history.
  - Only B1 lacks it, but B1's answer pleads standards compliance (Defense E).
  - Real memos about different devices and industries would not share this reasoning.
- **The "order of attack" paragraph recurs in all four memos.**
  - Files: A1/03 line 24, A2/03 §4 (line 28), B1/03 line 26, C1/03 line 30.
  - Each says to attack plaintiff's causation expert first, asks whether the opinion is "reliable and fits the facts", asks about testing or replication, and says narrowing causation makes the design theory "less relevant".
  - The phrasing is almost identical in A1 and C1.
- **All four email threads run the same negotiation.**
  - Plaintiff asks for the whole product family, defense limits to one model, plaintiff secures the "closest sibling" or a narrow carve-out, and defense calls it "not an admission".
  - Instances: A1 (AL-4125), A2 (AL-4130), B1 (TT-5500), C1 (JX-9 bearing shoulder).
  - The date logic of an expert exchange follows each time.
- **Telltale's own facts conflict between A1 and A2.**
  - Self-insured retention: A1/04 line 56 says $750,000 per claim (March 2025). A2/04 line 62 says $1,200,000 per claim (Sept 2024). It is the same GC, Marguerite Salvesen, and the same insurance liaison, Thaddeus Wren.
  - Expert-report review by the regulatory group: ten business days (A1/04 line 28) versus two weeks (A2/04 line 30). The group is led by the same named person, Odalys Fennimore.
  - Authority above $500,000 needs the CFO, Cormac Brightwater, in both.
  - Staffing: "two timekeepers at any deposition" versus "no more than three lawyers".
- **The firm acts for Telltale in two cases on the same ArcLine family, run by different partners and associates, without any mention of coordination.** A2/04 line 22 and guideline 6 bar sharing "with other counsel for Telltale". Neither file mentions an ethical wall for the firm's own other team.
- **Each client has a named insurance or risk contact.**
  - Examples: Thaddeus Wren (A1/A2), Delphine Moorcroft (B1), Perpetua Wallace (C1).
  - Each client also has an invented internal system (Orrinwick, Ravenscar, Corbelmark), mentioned once in a GC letter.
  - The pattern is one named system per client.
- **A "former employee witness" appears in two matters in the same sentence form.**
  - A2/03 line 18: "Plaintiff may also seek to depose Josefina Strathmore, a former Telltale supplier-quality auditor…".
  - C1/03 line 24: "Plaintiff may also seek to depose Hollis Pruett, a former Joad test technician…".
  - A1/03 line 16 has the anonymous "engineer working in the field" note. B1 has the retail-employee detail.
- **Every matter has an undisclosed adverse fact tucked into a paragraph about something else, in the same register.**
  - A1/03 lines 16 and 20 (the 2020 note, the Larkspur review).
  - A2/03 line 20 (supplier formula change).
  - B1/04 line 18 (retail partners), and B1/03 line 18 (resold box, ECN-6200-14).
  - C1/04 line 18 (supplier change), and C1/03 line 18 (tool mark).
  - These facts are unconnected to each other and are not carried through the later documents.
- **Each client instruction is echoed through three or four documents of the same matter.**
  - A1 and A2: "no offer until the treating cardiologist is deposed".
  - B1: mediation before experts.
  - C1: no authenticity stipulation without a protective order, and board approval above $1.5M.
  - In A2 and C1, opposing counsel even asks for or accepts the exact point.
- **Settlement structure is repeated.**
  - Several settlement gates cite $500,000 or $1.5M thresholds, each with a named approver.
  - The "three weeks" board lead time in C1 echoes the "ten business days" and "two weeks" review times in A1/A2.
- **Money and number habits repeat.**
  - Specials are round-ish and exposure is a range in every memo.
  - Budget ranges are quoted in every memo and letter.
  - Four client letters each state a budget ceiling that the memo's own figure then approaches or exceeds (A2 $450–600k vs $485–610k; A1 under $500k vs $410–520k).

2. A1/01 answer: realistic drafting notes ("Open drafting notes for partner"); wrong on "cleared" versus preemption and on numbering.
3. B1/01 answer: coherent, Texas-flavored, with a few non-defenses and a mixed general-denial structure.
4. A1/02 emails: a natural scope negotiation, spoiled only by the housekeeping insertion at line 71.
5. C1/02 emails: natural dates and logistics; the early authenticity request and the "I need to be direct" reply are the tell.
6. B1/02 emails: believable, but the line 35 client-policy disclosure and the consulting-expert request are off.
7. A2/01 answer: competent, with the odd serial-number admission and the answer to an amended complaint.
8. B1/03 memo: plausible numbers, but the ECN, resold-box and midpoint problems.
9. A1/04 letter: the most restrained of the four letters; the Orrinwick, inspection and regulatory-review items still stand out.
10. C1/03 memo: strong content, but the privileged-review sentence, the standards block and the restated instructions.
11. A1/03 memo: the Larkspur sentence and the standards block, plus the answer-timeline conflict.
12. A2/02 emails: the protective-order contradictions and the plaintiff-counsel statements against interest.
13. B1/04 letter: the retail-partner sentence, the policy repetition and the Ravenscar system.
14. C1/04 letter: the supplier disclosure, Corbelmark and the standards-body hint.
15. A2/03 memo: the supplier-formula sentence, the protective-order header, the mediation-statement conflation, the standards block and Section 8.
16. A2/04 letter: the $817,400 reserve, the "mediator should not learn" sentence and the conflicting retention figure.

## 5. What I could not assess

- Whether the fictional jurisdictions (Ashgrove, Linnet, Pelham Ridge, Tamarack) have rules that match what the documents say. I judged only against general US practice and a Texas-style reading of B1.
- Whether the product facts are credible: coil fatigue rates, insulation chemistry, pressure-cooker lockout mechanics, axle spindle metallurgy, or the cost figures and verdict ranges.
- Whether pacing leads in these facts would be PMA or 510(k) devices. I could only note that the files disagree on wording.
